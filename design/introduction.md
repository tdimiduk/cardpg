# caRdPG: Grounded Heroism

_caRdPG_ is a roleplaying game about grounded heroes, earned victories, and the stories forged when we push past our limits. In this game, your achievements are meaningful because they are born from your choices, your sacrifices, and the trials your characters endure.

---

### Grab Your Fate by the Edge

Traditional tabletop games ask you to sit passively while the Game Master rolls dice to see if a monster hits your character. _caRdPG_ rejects this passivity. Here, the defender is the active agent.

### Your Deck _is_ Your Character

In _caRdPG_, your hero is not defined by static numbers on a sheet. **Your character is a 24-card deck.** This deck is a living, breathing system that models three distinct aspects of your hero at once:

1. **Your Training and Capabilities:** The cards in your deck _are_ your skills, actions, and maneuvers. The attributes printed on them represent your physical and mental training.
2. **Your Tactical Presence of Mind:** The hand of cards you hold represents your immediate focus. It reflects the fleeting opportunities, sudden openings, and split-second decisions you can capitalize on in the heat of the moment.
3. **Your Endurance and Attrition:** The depth of your deck is your overall reserve. As you exert yourself, your deck degrades. Weariness and stress are represented by `Status Cards` (like `Fatigue`) that accumulate into your deck, clogging your hand and representing slipped concentration, heavy breathing, and fading focus.

### The Anatomy of Tension

Many modern games face a difficult trade-off: either characters can die at any moment to a sudden, lucky critical hit, or they feel completely invulnerable because they know the Game Master is protecting them behind the screen.

_caRdPG_ chooses a different path: **tension through informed risk.**

You will never be killed by a single, random die roll. Death in this game is not a sudden accident; it is a visible, telegraphed destination. As your character is battered, your deck clutters with Fatigue, and severe **Injuries** sit face-up on the table before you. The downward spiral is clear, tactile, and impossible to ignore.

The true danger enters when you _choose_ to ignore or push past the warning signs. If you are battered and exhausted, but decide to press on anyway to beat a ticking clock, explore deeper into a dungeon, or protect a friend, you are actively gambling with your hero's life. If you fall, it is not because of a cheap trick of the dice. It is the dramatic climax of a choice you made to accept escalating risk.

### Narrative Arcs of Recovery

When a challenge completely overwhelms your defenses, the result is an **Injury**. Injuries are not abstract points subtracted from a pool, nor are they permanent, crippling scars that ruin your character.

Instead, injuries are **narrative hurdles**. An injury is a physical card placed on the table in front of you—like a _Shattered Collarbone_ or a _Consuming Fever_ that temporarily changes how you play. It presents a new tactical problem for your entire party, forcing them to protect you, adapt their plans, and coordinate your defense.

Healing is not an instant magical reset button that erases the damage. Recovery is an involved narrative journey. Whether through mundane medicine or taxing magical spells, injuries are gradually _transformed_ into lesser burdens—like converting a major Wound into lingering Fatigue—before they are fully resolved. You _will_ fully recover eventually, but the process takes time, effort, and sanctuary, making your hard-won return to peak performance feel earned.

### The Tempo of Play

To support this style of storytelling, the game flows between two natural tempos:

- **Adventuring Time:** Used for travel, investigation, and downtime. Play is open-ended, and you maintain a small "Ready" hand face down on the table to react to sudden events.
- **Crisis Time:** Used when combat begins or a high-stakes hazard emerges. Play proceeds in rounds, but there is no waiting for your turn. Everyone plans secretly and acts simultaneously in a fast-paced tactical sprint.
