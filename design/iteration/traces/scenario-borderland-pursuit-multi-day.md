# Benchmark Scenario: Three Days on the Ash-Hound Trail

## Scenario Overview

This scenario traces a three-day, high-stakes overland pursuit across the sun-scorched, volcanic frontier known as the Ash-Wastes. A ruthless desert raider band—the Jackal-Cloaks—has ambushed a border diplomatic carriage, slaughtered the royal escort, stolen the irreplaceable Golden Treaty Scrolls, and abducted two youthful diplomatic hostages. They are fleeing toward their fortified redoubt: the ancient sandstone Watchtower of the Scythe, perched atop a razor-backed canyon three days' ride south.

The protagonists are a master wilderness scout (an expert tracker, hunter, and animal-handler) and a mounted highway marshal (a seasoned horseman, skirmisher, and tactical interrogator).

The journey unfolds over seventy-two continuous hours:

- **Day 1:** Relentless tracking across caustic alkali flats, managing horse hydration, foraging sparse desert scrub, and making a defensible perimeter camp under cold desert stars.
- **Day 2:** Navigating the sole crossing of the yawning Black Gorge: a fortified cable-ferry waystation operated by corrupt river-scum secretly allied with the raiders, demanding social deception, sleight of hand, and subtle subversion.
- **Day 3 (Dawn):** A stealthy approach through sandstone gullies culminating in a fast, explosive dawn assault on the watchtower redoubt to liberate the hostages before the raiders can sacrifice or execute them.

This scenario serves as the primary benchmark for **multi-day macro attrition**, **what a night's camp clears versus what persists across days**, and **how social failure propagates forward as tactical enemy readiness rather than personal physical impairment**.

## Day 1: Relentless Pursuit Across the Ash-Wastes

### Narrative Context & Stakes

The chase begins under the blistering mid-morning sun. The Ash-Wastes are an expanse of blinding white alkali pans, treacherous sinkholes, and jagged basalt ridges. The raiders have a six-hour head start; if the pursuers lose the spoor or founder their mounts, the raiders will reach the safety of the watchtower and seal the canyon passes behind them.

The scout and marshal ride hard, trading speed against the biological limits of their horses. The scout reads broken thorn-twigs, horse droppings, and dust flurries, following hunter's instinct when the trail vanishes across hard rock. When the marshal’s gelding is spooked by a venomous pit-viper and nearly bolts into a ravine, the marshal uses iron hands to calm and control the beast.

As the sun sets, freezing desert winds sweep the flats. The pair halts at a sheltered dry wash: foraging scrub brush for fuel, checking hooves for stone-bruises, rationing alkali-filtered water, and setting trip-snares around the perimeter before taking alternating four-hour watches under the stars.

### Actions & Colors Tagged

- `Maneuver and Pursue` ([[Yellow]] - Exploration): Pacing horses over treacherous shale and alkali flats, maintaining rapid pursuit speed without blowing their wind.
- `Forage and Survive` ([[Red]] - Exploration): Locating an underground desert seep to replenish water skins and finding edible scrub roots in an arid wasteland.
- `Follow an Instinct` ([[Red]] - Exploration): Trusting a seasoned hunter’s gut feeling to cut across a dangerous basalt saddle, betting that the raiders skirted the ridge.
- `Tame a Beast` ([[Red]] - Exploration): Calming and dominating a panicked, rearing warhorse when it encounters desert predators.
- `Claim Territory` ([[Red]] - Exploration): Marking camp boundaries with scent and fire-brands to intimidate nocturnal desert ash-wolves into keeping their distance.
- `Set up Camp` ([[Yellow]] - Exploration): Building a concealed, wind-sheltered bivouac in a dry wash, stringing perimeter warning bells and trip-wires.

### Pacing Mode

`General Action (Adventuring Time)`. A full twelve-hour operational day broken into travel segments and an evening camp. Success in tracking is maintained through professional diligence; the resolution determines how much travel weariness accumulates on riders and mounts, how much water is preserved, and how restful the night camp proves to be.

### Plausible Narrative Fallout Spectrum

- **Clean Outcome:** Perfect tracking. The party cuts two hours off the raiders’ lead; horses are watered and sound; clean bivouac established; full four-hour sleep shifts restore universal daily stamina.
- **Minor Friction:** Arid travel friction. A horse throws an iron shoe, requiring field-nailing; alkali dust parches throats and irritates eyes (`Irritated Eyes / Dust Cough`); mounts are tired; sleep is restless due to howling ash-wolves; minor stiffness on waking.
- **Severe Consequence:** Crippling overland setback. The gelding steps into a hidden sinkhole, wrenching its hock (`Lame Mount`); riders suffer agonizing chafing and back spasms from twelve hours of continuous hard riding (`Severe Saddle-Sore Exhaustion`); party loses the spoor in the dark, wasting hours refinding the trail at dawn.

### Engine Brainstorming Prompts: Coupling & Cross-Domain Bleed

1. **What Camp Cleanses vs. What Persists Across Days:**
   - In a grounded heroic adventure, a night's sleep around a warm fire with hot rations flushes **routine universal fatigue** (the general tiredness of riding, watching, and worrying).
   - But what about **structural and physiological conditions**?
     - Does a night's sleep cure a horse's sprained hock (`Lame Mount`)? No.
     - Does it cure raw, bleeding saddle-sores or a wrenched knee? No.
     - Does it cure an exhausted watchman who stayed awake six hours because wolves circled the camp?
   - How should an engine differentiate between **fleeting fatigue** (which flushes at every routine camp) and **persistent travel burdens** (which cling to riders and gear across multi-day journeys)?
2. **Mount and Gear Degradation:**
   - In wilderness pursuit, a hero’s capability is intimately tied to their mount. If a horse is lame, does the condition attach to the rider’s hand or to a dedicated mount card on the table? If the mount is slowed, how does that translate into lost ground against the quarry?

## Day 2: The Corrupt Ferry Waystation & The Gorge Crossing

### Narrative Context & Stakes

Midday of the second day. The trail leads directly to the Black River Gorge: a sheer, 300-foot chasm through which the swollen Black River roars. The only crossing within twenty leagues is the Cable-Ferry at Crow’s Perch: a massive timber raft suspended from iron cables, operated by a fortified waystation on the near bluff.

The raiders crossed five hours ago. The waystation is manned by Old Barnaby—a cynical, one-eyed ferryman—and a dozen rough cutthroats who are on the Jackal-Cloaks’ payroll. Barnaby claims the ferry’s main iron winch-spool has sheared its pin and will take twelve hours to repair. The heroes know this is a deliberate delay to allow the raiders to reach the watchtower unmolested.

Barnaby’s men are armed with crossbows along the waystation roof. A frontal assault would risk the ferry cable being cut, stranding the pursuers forever. The heroes must play a dangerous game: the marshal uses forged transit letters, merchant slang, and fast-talking bribes to distract Barnaby inside the taproom, reading the ferryman’s lips as he whispers orders to an underling. Meanwhile, the scout sneaks into the kennel yard, uses sleep-herbs to quietly pacify the ferocious guard hounds, and performs delicate sleight of hand to free the jammed winch lock.

### Actions & Colors Tagged

- `Con and Deceive` ([[Yellow]] - Social): Passing off forged frontier passes and weaving a convincing cover story of urgent gold-shipment escorts to lower Barnaby’s guard.
- `Lip-Read` ([[Yellow]] - Social): Reading Barnaby’s lips across the smoky tavern as he whispers to a boy to release carrier pigeons to the watchtower.
- `Gain Leverage` ([[Blue]] - Social): Deducing Barnaby’s fear of the royal excise inspectors and quietly threatening him with exposure to force cooperation.
- `Perform Sleight of Hand` ([[Yellow]] - Social / Exploration): Slipping the locking pin out of the ferry winch mechanism unseen while pretending to inspect rope tackle.
- `Subtle Poisoning` ([[Yellow]] - Social): Tossing dried soporific tallow balls into the kennel pens to drug the vicious watchdog pack into silent sleep.
- `Employ a Specific Bane` ([[Yellow]] - Exploration): Using sulfur smoke to drive nesting hornets into the roof-watchers' roost, creating chaos without drawing steel.

### Pacing Mode

`General Action (Adventuring Time)`. High-tension covert and social infiltration. The characters are operating in plain sight under the noses of hostile cutthroats; the stakes are stealth, time, and avoiding an open firefight that would sever the cable.

### Plausible Narrative Fallout Spectrum

- **Clean Outcome:** Flawless deception and silent crossing. Barnaby is completely taken in by the disguise; the guard hounds sleep; the winch is freed without a sound; the ferry glides across the gorge in twenty minutes; Barnaby waves them off, unaware he has helped the law.
- **Minor Friction:** Costly bribery and heightened suspicion. Barnaby demands an outrageous toll in gold and the marshal’s spare horse; crossing takes two hours; Barnaby remains uneasy and posts a rider to watch the southern ridge.
- **Severe Consequence:** Blown cover and alerted destination. Barnaby spots the sleight of hand at the winch; he realizes who they are and releases a carrier pigeon to the Watchtower before the scout can shoot it down; the heroes are forced to draw steel, fight their way onto the ferry, and winch themselves across under crossbow fire; they cross, but **the raiders at the watchtower now know pursuers are coming**.

### Engine Brainstorming Prompts: Coupling & Cross-Domain Bleed

1. **The Crucial Asymmetric Bleed Principle: Social/Cover Failure as Tactical Enemy Readiness:**
   - If the heroes fail their deception in Beat 2 and blow their cover, **how should that consequence manifest in Day 3?**
   - **What it does NOT do:**
     - Blowing cover at the ferry does NOT break the marshal’s sword arm.
     - It does NOT make the scout’s arrows fly crooked.
     - It does NOT reduce their physical stamina, muscle power, or dodge speed.
   - **What it DOES do:**
     - The carrier pigeon reaches the Watchtower hours ahead of the heroes.
     - When the heroes arrive in Day 3, the raiders have barricaded the iron doors, manned the arrow slits, set pitch cauldrons on the parapet, and tied the hostages to the outer wall as human shields.
   - **The Engine Imperative:** The engine must clearly distinguish between **internal conditions** (wounds, exhaustion, mental shock) and **external environmental/tactical conditions** (enemy alertness, fortified positions, lost surprise).
   - When social actions fail, the fallout should propagate into the world's tactical setup, not into personal bodily rot!

## Day 3 (Dawn): Stealth Infiltration & The Watchtower Assault

### Narrative Context & Stakes

Dawn of the third day. The sun rises like molten bronze over the sandstone bluffs. The Watchtower of the Scythe rises eighty feet above the red-rock ravines: an ancient cyclopean stone tower with high arrow slits and a walled courtyard. The raiders have corralled their stolen horses in the yard; smoke rises from a cookfire atop the parapet.

Inside the tower, the raider chieftain prepares to execute the royal diplomats to seal their pact with rival lords. The time for caution has run out.

The heroes utilize the morning glare and sandstone arroyos to slip past the outer canyon sentries. Once in position outside the courtyard gate, they launch an explosive, high-violence dawn assault: the scout puts precision arrows through key sentinels on the catwalks while the marshal leads a ferocious charge through the palisade, exploiting structural weaknesses in the decayed sandstone wall to breach the inner sanctum and shield the hostages from execution.

### Actions & Colors Tagged

- `Move Unseen` ([[Yellow]] - Exploration): Crawling through dust ravines and shadow pockets under the morning sun to reach the watchtower base without alerting the lookout.
- `Lead a Ferocious Charge` ([[Red]] - Combat): Smashing through the timber courtyard gates with warhorse and spear, scattering the raider breakfast line with shock violence.
- `Attack with Precision` ([[Yellow]] - Combat): Dropping the raider executioner with a fast arrow shot through the slit of the upper watchroom door.
- `Exploit a Weakness` ([[Blue]] - Combat): Recognizing that the ancient sandstone parapet is structurally undermined and toppling a heavy stone water-trough to crush the inner door barricade.
- `Make a Last Stand` ([[Red]] - Combat): Throwing oneself between three charging raider champions and the bound hostages, holding the interior doorway with sword and buckler until help arrives.

### Pacing Mode

`General Action (Approach)` transitioning seamlessly into `Crisis (Crisis Time) (The Assault)`. A sequence demonstrating how an approach phase sets the initial tactical parameters of a life-or-death combat crisis.

### Plausible Narrative Fallout Spectrum

- **Clean Outcome (Assault with Full Surprise):** (Assumes Day 2 succeeded cleanly). Total tactical shock. Sentinels fall silently; the charge scatters the raiders before they can reach their weapons; the chieftain is cut down in his bed; the hostages and Treaty Scrolls are recovered completely intact with zero hero wounds.
- **Minor Friction (Tense Breaching Action):** Hostages secured, but the chieftain rallies his veteran guard in the spiral stairwell; a brutal five-minute melee inflicts sharp cuts and bruises (`Lacerated Arm / Bruised Ribs`); the Treaty Scrolls are slightly scorched before being snatched from the hearth fire.
- **Severe Consequence (Assault Against an Alerted Fortress):** (Direct result of Day 2’s Blown Cover). The raiders were waiting on the walls. The approach is met with crossbow fire; the marshal’s mount is killed beneath them; the heroes must breach under burning pitch; the marshal makes a desperate last stand taking grievous wounds (`Crossbow Bolt to the Shoulder / Severe Burns`); the hostages are saved by a hair's breadth as the tower catches fire.

### Engine Brainstorming Prompts: Coupling & Cross-Domain Bleed

1. **Three Days of Cumulative Travel Attrition in Crisis:**
   - The heroes have spent seventy-two hours in the saddle, sleeping on cold dirt, eating dry pemmican, and breathing alkali dust.
   - When the adrenaline of the dawn assault hits, how does that accumulated multi-day fatigue manifest?
   - Does it reduce their initial stamina pool? Does it mean that once the combat ends, they will immediately collapse from sheer exhaustion?
2. **Seamless Transition from Stealth Approach to Violent Crisis:**
   - The scene begins with `Move Unseen` (an exploration general action) and detonates into `Lead a Ferocious Charge` (a combat crisis).
   - How does the resolution engine handle this transition? Does an exceptional stealth approach grant bonus momentum or surprise advantage in the opening combat round? Does blowing stealth automatically trigger Crisis Time with the enemies having higher defense?

## Summary Evaluation Rubric for Engine Designers

When validating a candidate resolution engine against this scenario, ensure it answers these core design questions cleanly:

| Invariant                          | Design Challenge                                                                                                              | Narrative Test                                                                                                              |
| :--------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Multi-Day Rest Differentiation** | Does camp rest clear routine universal fatigue while leaving bodily injuries and mount conditions persistent?                 | Does a night around the fire refresh a tired marshal's energy while leaving their gelding's sprained hock unhealed?         |
| **Cover Blown $\ne$ Bodily Rot**   | Does failing a social deception at the ferry avoid penalizing the heroes' physical sword skill or health?                     | Does Barnaby spotting a fake pass result in alerted guards at the destination rather than making the heroes easier to stab? |
| **External Consequence Tracking**  | Can the engine track consequences attached to the environment, enemies, or mounts rather than just player decks?              | Do `Alerted Fortress` or `Lame Mount` exist as clear, distinct operational conditions outside the PC's body?                |
| **Pacing Handoff**                 | Does an exploration approach (`Move Unseen`) transition naturally into a fast, violent Crisis without awkward rules friction? | Does crawling up a gully cleanly establish the starting distance and surprise advantage of the subsequent assault?          |
| **Mount and Vehicle Dependency**   | Does the engine treat mounts and transport as vital operational assets subject to stamina, trauma, and care?                  | Does losing a horse or having a mount tire out create real strategic tension in an overland chase?                          |
