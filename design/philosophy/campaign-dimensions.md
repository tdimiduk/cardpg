# Campaign Dimensions: Curating the Experience

This document outlines the philosophy and practical calibration of **Campaign Dimensions** in _caRdPG_. The core engine operates on an invariant, physically grounded substrate, but campaigns can vary widely in feel, stakes, and complexity. Rather than locking the game into a single genre, the engine spans a multi-dimensional design space governed by three independent **dimensions of variation**:

1. **Scale & Power Level:** The numerical advancement ceiling and scope of action.
2. **Supernatural Presence:** The ubiquity, power, and nature of magic and wonder.
3. **Consequence Rigor & Recovery:** The permanence of harm, speed of healing, and trajectory of character growth.

To illustrate the span of this design space, this document maps out **three canonical reference points**: _Grim Simulationist_, _Grounded Heroism (our default flagship)_, and _Kid-Friendly Adventure_.

---

## 1. A Unified Physical Substrate

Our design approach begins with a consistent, neutral physical substrate grounded in our verisimilitude research: realistic momentum, thermodynamics, biological limits, metabolic expenditure, and the physical behavior of materials.

Crucially, **this physical substrate is tonally neutral**. It is not an aesthetic genre or a mandate for misery. Physics applies equally to a sunlit alpine meadow as it does to a sodden peat bog; it governs the flight of a falcon just as strictly as the strike of an infected blade.

The **Grim Simulationist** reference point is not the source code of the world—it is merely one specific configuration that couples this physical substrate with themes of despair, cynical exhaustion, and inevitable decay. Our flagship baseline, **Grounded Heroism**, uses the exact same physical laws, but pairs them with **Dynamic Range across all narrative axes**: heroic agency, wonder, beauty, camaraderie, and earned triumph. Realism does not exist to punish the players; it exists as an honest anchor that makes their achievements, sacrifices, and discoveries feel authentic.

---

## 2. Core Design Tenet: Dynamic Range Across All Axes

A central pitfall in game design—especially when incorporating empirical verisimilitude—is flattening the emotional palette into monotone grey mud. To feel vibrant, a campaign must maintain **dynamic range across all axes**:

- **Light and Shadow (Atmospheric Dynamic Range):** Dread and peril carry weight only because there is warmth, sanctuary, fellowship, and beauty worth fighting for. An ancient ruin feels solemn and tragic only if the lost civilization once had majesty. Gloom without light is not dramatic; it is merely tedious.
- **Wonder and Physical Grounding (Magical Dynamic Range):** Wonder hits harder _because_ the physical world has rules. When supernatural marvels, ancient relics, or sacred miracles appear, they feel awe-inspiring precisely because players understand the stubborn gravity of the mundane world. At the same time, we avoid the **Ubiquitous Grandeur Trap**: if every forest glitters and every stone sparkles with fairy dust, wonder becomes cheap wallpaper. Wonder is earned, context-appropriate, and rooted in the scene.
- **Harsh Survival Where It Is the Plot (Pacing Dynamic Range):** If a scenario is explicitly about braving a howling blizzard, surviving a desert crossing, or escaping a collapsed, airless mine, the raw physical realities of exposure, thirst, and biological limits _are_ the drama. But survival mechanics serve specific dramatic situations; they must not become an oppressive, universal tax levied on routine travel or civilized hearths.
- **Moral and Adversarial Breadth (Dramatic Dynamic Range):** The world is not locked into an endless wash of cynical moral grey. There is room for **dastardly, unapologetic villains** and terrifying apex beasts when clear, high-stakes opposition drives the story forward—alongside **complex, multi-faceted factions** and tragic dilemmas when players choose to engage in political or social intrigue.
- **Heroic Competence and Genuine Cost:** Protagonists are capable, skilled adventurers. They know how to bank a hearth, secure their footing, and negotiate treacherous ground without tumbling into slapstick incompetence. The "grounded" quality does not mean heroes are fragile victims; it means that when they face legendary perils and push beyond human limits, the costs (tactical wear, accumulated fatigue, and injuries) are real and tangible rather than brushed aside by plot armor.

### The Two Guardrails: Avoiding the Extremes

```
[ Grimdark Collapse ] <======== [ GROUNDED HEROISM ] ========> [ Ubiquitous Grandeur ]
  Endless brown mud,              Dynamic range across all       Effortless sparkles,
  sepsis, flash rust,             axes; real physics, earned     consequence-free magic,
  hopeless attrition              triumph, contextual wonder     cartoon invulnerability
```

1. **Guardrail 1: The Grimdark Collapse.** The mistake of believing that realism requires everything to be wet, rotting, rusty, infected, desperate, and miserable.
2. **Guardrail 2: The High-Fantasy Gloss / Ubiquitous Grandeur.** The mistake of saturating the world with effortless, consequence-free magic and glittering spectacle until physical consequence dissolves and wonder loses all impact.

## 3. The Three Campaign Dimensions

Curating a _caRdPG_ campaign involves positioning the game along three independent dimensions of variation:

### Dimension 1: Scale & Power Level (Numerical Scale)

A campaign's **Power Level** defines the overall scope and scale of the characters, the magnitude of challenges they face, and the **advancement ceiling** of card numbers:

- **Numerical Advancement as a Dimension:** In _caRdPG_, character advancement is driven by the **numerical scaling of cards** (upgrading card color values, defenses, and modifiers in the 24-card deck) alongside acquiring new tactical stances and actions. The dimension determines how far those numbers advance:
  - **Bounded / Low Power (Single Digits):** Card values stay tightly bounded in the single digits (values 1–5, peaking around 8–10). Even a +1 card upgrade represents a career milestone. Mortal biological limits remain central throughout the campaign.
  - **Heroic / High Power (Double Digits):** Characters scale steadily into double-digit card numbers (values 10–50+), allowing them to directly confront mythical beasts, colossal environmental hazards, and small garrisons.
  - **Exalted / Anime Tier (Triple Digits):** In rare, extremely high-power settings, individual hero cards can scale into triple digits (100–500+), enabling characters to act as living forces of nature capable of sundering terrain and shattering legions.
- **The Scope of Action & Scale of Threats:** A low-power character influences a village and treats a single armored knight as a lethal boss; a high-power character topples dynasties and trades blows with dragons.
- **Fractal Scope: Macro-Entity Decks (Armies, Provinces, Kingdoms):** The core engine's scale invariance means high numbers are not reserved only for mythic heroes. A deck can represent an entire **army**, a **province**, or an **empire**. Giant numbers on macro cards represent the coordinated manpower, pooled wealth, and collective logistics of thousands of people. Mass warfare and geopolitical campaigns resolve using the exact same Crisis Time and General Action mechanics as a tavern skirmish, providing smooth abstracted resolution at any narrative scale.

### Dimension 2: Supernatural Presence (Magic & Wonder)

The presence and nature of the supernatural is an independent dimension from power level and tone:

- **Mundane / Low Wonder:** Magic is subtle, ancient, forgotten, or completely non-existent. A single enchanted heirloom or ancestral curse carries generations of folklore. Challenges are resolved through steel, cunning, and endurance.
- **Grounded Wonder (Default):** The supernatural exists as a tangible, wondrous reality with distinct laws, costs, and natural ecology. Miracles, relics, and spells are real and potent, providing heroic tools that interact directly with the physical substrate (e.g., mending grievous wounds, warding against shadows) without trivializing logistics or eliminating physical fatigue.
- **Vibrant / Mythic Ecology:** Magic is woven into the living fabric of the world—enchanted forests, guardian spirits, alchemical fauna, and elder ruins humming with latent energy. Magic remains grounded in consistent rules and visceral costs, but wonder is an active part of the landscape.

### Dimension 3: Consequence Rigor & Recovery (Attrition & Advancement)

The third dimension defines the weight of physical trauma, the speed and permanence of recovery, and the mathematical shape of character advancement:

- **Irreversible Attrition (Non-Monotonic Progression):** Consequences are severe, traumatic, and frequently permanent. The default Consequence Deck uses full prognosis rules (e.g., compound fractures, sepsis, lingering limps). Healing is slow and incomplete. Characters grow through hard-won experience, but simultaneously accumulate permanent scars and debuffs; advancement is non-monotonic.
- **Narrative Recovery Arcs (Monotonic Progression - Default):** Serious injuries are significant tactical hurdles—modeled by Layered Condition Cards—but are fully treatable through medical lore, miracles, and downtime. Treatment converts severe wounds into temporary [[Fatigue]]. Characters advance upward monotonically; physical harm produces dramatic chapter hurdles, not permanent physical rot.
- **Ephemeral Tactical Disadvantage (Scene-Bounded):** Consequences are framed as immediate, short-term tactical penalties (e.g., `Staggered`, `Disarmed`, `Pinned Down`) that clear automatically when the scene concludes. [[Fatigue]] resets after a brief rest. Advancement is purely positive and additive.

## 4. Canonical Reference Points in Design Space

To demonstrate how these three dimensions combine to curate specific experiences, the engine defines three canonical reference points:

---

### Reference Point 1: Grim Simulationist

- **Core Concept & Inspirations:** Models the harsh realities of a pre-modern world where survival is the primary victory. Adheres strictly to empirical research on pre-modern trauma and hardship.
- **Inspirations:** _Darkest Dungeon_, _Torchbearer_, The Black Company Series, Berserk, Alien (1979).
- **Thematic Intent:** To capture a tone of costly attrition and psychological toll, where protagonists are worn down by their struggles rather than purely empowered.
- **Narrative Focus:** Survival, attrition, the high cost of violence. A "successful" character is one who lives to retire, often permanently scarred.
- **Dimensional Calibration:**
  - **Power Level:** Bounded / Low Power (Single Digits).
  - **Supernatural Presence:** Mundane / Low Wonder. Magic, if present, carries severe risks and unpredictable complications.
  - **Consequence Rigor:** Irreversible Attrition. Condition Cards use full prognosis text (`Compound Fracture`, `Sepsis`). Non-monotonic advancement trade-offs.

---

### Reference Point 2: Grounded Heroism (Default Flagship)

- **Core Concept & Inspirations:** The game's intended default experience. Balances rigorous physical reality with heroic fantasy, dynamic range, and earned wonder. Characters are competent, courageous protagonists defined by their ability to overcome formidable odds against an unyielding, physically authentic world.
- **Detailed Guide:** See [narrative-baseline.md](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/philosophy/narrative-baseline.md) for the core narrative invariants, tactical dilemmas, adventure lifecycle, and scenario evaluation rubric for this reference point.
- **Inspirations:**
  - **Literary Canon:** _The Lord of the Rings_ (J.R.R. Tolkien), _The Deed of Paksenarrion_ (Elizabeth Moon), _The Wheel of Time_ (Robert Jordan), _The Stormlight Archive & Mistborn_ (Brandon Sanderson), _The Heralds of Valdemar_ (Mercedes Lackey), _The Honor Harrington Series_ (David Weber), _The Earthsea Cycle_ (Ursula K. Le Guin), _Dungeon Meshi_ (Ryōko Kui), _The Witcher Series_ (Andrzej Sapkowski), _A Song of Ice and Fire_ (George R.R. Martin).
  - **Cinematic & Anime Canon:** _Princess Mononoke_ & _Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind_ (Studio Ghibli), _Frieren: Beyond Journey's End_, _Seven Samurai_ & Classical Samurai Cinema (Akira Kurosawa), _The Mummy (1999)_ / _Indiana Jones_.
  - **Interactive CRPG Canon:** _Baldur's Gate (1–3)_, _The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim_, _Dragon Age: Origins_.
- **Thematic Intent:** To model heroism defined by courage, purpose, and earned victory. Protagonists operate within realistic physical boundaries, but the world holds beauty, companionship, ancient wonder, and noble stakes.
- **Narrative Focus:** Earned victories, perseverance, facing meaningful consequences, and dynamic adventures. A grievous wound is a dramatic narrative recovery arc—a temporary hurdle that rallies the party—rather than a permanent crippling penalty.
- **Dimensional Calibration:**
  - **Power Level:** Heroic Tier (Double Digits, 10–50+).
  - **Supernatural Presence:** Grounded Wonder. Tangible magic governed by kinetic, metabolic, or spiritual conservation laws.
  - **Consequence Rigor:** Narrative Recovery Arcs. Traumatic injuries are treatable and convert to [[Fatigue]]. Characters advance monotonically.

#### The Grounded Heroic Canon: How Our Touchstones Frame Play

1. **Military Realism Without Moral Cynicism (_The Deed of Paksenarrion_):** Elizabeth Moon demonstrates that grueling march discipline, chainmail maintenance, blister management, and clinical wound recovery do not require cynicism, moral relativism, or despair. Paks suffers real trauma and physical exhaustion, but her story is one of genuine honor, fellowship, divine calling, and earned paladinhood. Real physics does not demand moral rot.
2. **Duty, Tactical Stances, & Hard Costs (_The Wheel of Time_):** Robert Jordan grounds martial combat in named tactical forms and stances ("The Boar Rushes Down the Mountain", "Heron Spreads Its Wings") that directly parallel _caRdPG_'s Stances and Action Cards. Magic carries strict biological fatigue and limits. Extreme dynamic range: cozy Two Rivers warmth contrasts with terrifying Trolloc raids.
3. **Competence Governed by Physical Law (_Honor Harrington_ / David Weber):** Newton’s laws, acceleration curves, sensor lag, and damage control are non-negotiable. Characters win through preparation, doctrine, and courage within the laws of the universe. Honor suffers severe physical trauma, yet remains an inspiring leader facing both dastardly villains and honorable rivals.
4. **The Sanctuary & Convalescence Cycle (_The Heralds of Valdemar_ / Mercedes Lackey):** Healing is not a sterile numerical reset. Physical and emotional recovery is an active narrative pillar involving warm hearths, companionship, music, herbal care, and patience. This informs _caRdPG_'s downtime and transformation of Wounds into Fatigue.
5. **Hard Magic as Kinetic Conservation (_The Stormlight Archive_ / Brandon Sanderson):** Magic follows clear conservation rules, operating much like a card economy. Crucially, grueling physical labor (Bridge Four carrying massive timbers under fire) becomes the forge for noble oaths ("Life before death, strength before weakness"), camaraderie, and epic triumph.
6. **The Adventuring Rhythm & Environmental Contrast (_Baldur's Gate_, _Skyrim_, _Dragon Age: Origins_):** These games achieve dynamic range through their pacing loop: transitioning naturally from the warm hearth of a roadside inn to a wind-blasted mountain pass, the haunting silence of a subterranean crypt, and the comfort of a campsite shared with companions.

### Reference Point 3: Kid-Friendly Adventure

- **Core Concept & Inspirations:** A lighter, whimsical tone suitable for all ages. The focus is on creative problem-solving and personal growth without the grim realities of violence.
- **Inspirations:** _Avatar: The Last Airbender_, Studio Ghibli films, Might and Magic Series, The Hobbit, The Chronicles of Narnia.
- **Thematic Intent:** To provide models for non-lethal conflict and character advancement centered on friendship and learning new skills, rather than overcoming violent threats.
- **Narrative Focus:** Friendship, learning, helping others. "Defeat" is a temporary, low-stakes setback that provides a lesson for the future.
- **Dimensional Calibration:**
  - **Power Level:** Heroic Tier with forgiving scaling.
  - **Supernatural Presence:** Vibrant & Wondrous. Magic is accessible, colorful, and a primary tool for creative problem-solving.
  - **Consequence Rigor:** Ephemeral Tactical Disadvantage. Consequences are simple conditions (`Staggered`, `Pinned Down`) that expire at scene end. Fatigue resets on short rests. Purely positive advancement.
