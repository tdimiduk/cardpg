# Armor Protection Profiles

This document summarizes the historical and physiological characteristics of core armor types. Ratings are on a 0-10 scale, where 10 represents the pinnacle of protection.

* Spatial Distribution: The armor's ability to spread impact force over a wide area (provided by rigid components).

* Temporal Distribution: The armor's ability to cushion a blow by increasing its duration (provided by soft components).

---
## Padded Doublet (Gambeson)

* Description: A thick, quilted jacket made from multiple layers of fabric.

* Typical Weight: 6-12 lbs (3-5.5 kg).

* Fatigue Mechanisms: Primarily severe heat insulation.

* Typical Coverage (4/10): Torso, arms, and upper thighs.

* Force Distribution Analysis: Relies purely on Temporal Distribution (cushioning). It has no spatial distribution.

* Injury Prevention Analysis: Excellent at turning moderate cuts into minor bruises, but its lack of rigidity means it offers little protection against truly severe, focused force.

## Leather Jack (Cuir Bouilli)

* Description: Armor made from leather hardened into a rigid cuirass.

* Typical Weight: 8-15 lbs (3.5-7 kg).

* Fatigue Mechanisms: Poor breathability and rigidity.

* Typical Coverage (3/10): Primarily the torso.

* Force Distribution Analysis: Relies on Spatial Distribution, but from a brittle material prone to failure under heavy, focused blows. It offers no tempor cushioning.**

* Injury Prevention Analysis: Its rigidity makes it great at deflecting or nullifying minor impacts. However, it is very poor at preventing moderate or serious injurie as a blow that overcomes its structural integrity meets little remaining resistance.**

## Maille Hauberk

* Description: A shirt of interlocking rings, worn over a Padded Doublet.

* Typical Weight: 20-30 lbs (9-13.5 kg), *plus* the gambeson.

* Fatigue Mechanisms: Significant weight with poor shoulder-based distribution.

* Typical Coverage (7/10): Torso, arms, and upper legs.

* Force Distribution Analysis: A composite system. The maille provides a hard outer layer excellent at defeating cuts, while the gambeson provides Temporal Distribution for the secondary impact. It lacks significant spatial distribution against blunt force.

* Injury Prevention Analysis: Excellent at preventing minor and moderate skin-breaking wounds (cuts). Its weakness is that the concussive force of heavy blows  transmitted through the armor, making it poor at preventing serious structural injuries like broken bones.

## Brigandine

* Description: A vest with overlapping steel plates riveted inside, worn over padding and/or maille.

* Typical Weight: 15-25 lbs (7-11 kg), plus under-layers.

* Fatigue Mechanisms: Significant weight and heat from layering.

* Typical Coverage (6/10): Primarily the torso.

* Force Distribution Analysis: A highly effective composite system using both Spatial Distribution from its many steel plates and Temporal Distribution from its padded foundation.

* Injury Prevention Analysis: Represents a major leap in protection. The internal plates are very effective at stopping all but the most powerful and well-aimed attacks from causing serious harm.

## Lamellar Armor

* Description: Small plates laced together into flexible, overlapping rows, worn over padding.

* Typical Weight: 25-35 lbs (11-16 kg).

* Fatigue Mechanisms:  Significant weight.

* Typical Coverage (8/10): Often constructed to be very comprehensive.

* Force Distribution Analysis: Similar to Brigandine, it leverages both Spatial Distribution from its laminated plates and Temporal Distribution from its padding and flexible lacing.

* Injury Prevention Analysis: An excellent and balanced defensive system, very similar in performance to Brigandine but often providing more complete coverage.

## Full Plate Harness

* Description: A complete, head-to-toe suit of interlocking and articulating steel plates.

* Typical Weight: 45-70 lbs (20-32 kg).

* Fatigue Mechanisms: High weight, heat buildup, and severe respiratory restriction.

* Typical Coverage (10/10): The benchmark for complete protection.

* Force Distribution Analysis: The pinnacle of both principles. It offers unmatched Spatial Distribution due to its large, rigid, perfectly shaped plates, and effective Temporal Distribution from the underlying arming doublet.

* Injury Prevention Analysis: The most effective form of personal protection ever created. It makes minor and moderate injuries almost impossible and is exceptionally resilient to attacks that would cause serious harm to a lesser-armored individual.
