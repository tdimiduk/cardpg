# Appendix: Master Source Index

*A curated list of literary, cinematic, and gaming sources used to inspire the design of game actions, themes, and mechanics.*

## Literary, Cinematic, & Mythological Sources

* ***Epic Western Fantasy***
  * ***The Lord of the Rings** by J.R.R. Tolkien*
  * ***A Song of Ice and Fire** by George R.R. Martin*
  * ***The Witcher Saga** by Andrzej Sapkowski*
  * ***The Black Company Series** by Glen Cook*
* ***Sword & Sorcery Literature***
  * ***Robert E. Howard** (Conan the Barbarian)*
  * ***Fritz Leiber** (Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser)*
  * ***Michael Moorcock** (Elric of Melniboné)*
* ***Kid-Friendly & Whimsical Literature***
  * ***The Hobbit** by J.R.R. Tolkien*
  * ***The Chronicles of Narnia** by C.S. Lewis*
  * ***The Harry Potter Series** by J.K. Rowling*
  * ***The Redwall Series** by Brian Jacques*
  * ***Howl's Moving Castle** by Diana Wynne Jones*
* ***Mythology & Epic Poems***
  * ***Arthurian Legend:** The tales of King Arthur and the Knights of the Round Table.*
  * ***Greek & Mesopotamian Epics** such as The Odyssey and the Epic of Gilgamesh.*
  * ***Norse Sagas:** The heroic and mythological tales of Viking culture.*
* ***Japanese Folklore & Literature***
  * ***Shinto & Buddhist Mythology:** Including the rich folklore of Kami (spirits/gods), Yokai (monsters), and Yurei (ghosts).*
  * ***The Tale of Genji:** A classic of courtly life, providing deep insight into aesthetics, subtle social maneuvering, and personal relationships.*
  * ***Musashi** by Eiji Yoshikawa: An epic on the philosophy of swordsmanship, discipline, and the life of a wandering warrior.*
* ***Chinese Folklore & Literature***
  * ***Journey to the West:** A foundational epic of pilgrimage, divine tests, and trickster heroes.*
  * ***Romance of the Three Kingdoms:** The quintessential epic of warfare, statecraft, personal honor, and grand strategy.*
  * ***Water Margin:** A classic tale of a growing band of 108 outlaws, providing a blueprint for faction-based play and diverse character archetypes.*
  * ***The Wuxia Genre:** The tradition of chivalrous martial artists who achieve superhuman feats, offering a model for non-magical but powerful action.*
* ***Genre Films & Television***
  * ***Samurai Cinema** (e.g., Kurosawa's Seven Samurai, Yojimbo)*
  * ***Studio Ghibli Films** (e.g., Princess Mononoke, Spirited Away)*
  * ***Mushishi** (Anime/Manga): A masterclass in non-combat, exploratory problem-solving focused on understanding and resolving supernatural phenomena.*
  * ***Berserk** (Anime/Manga): A key reference for themes of enduring hardship, fighting against overwhelming odds, and a gritty, dark fantasy tone.*
  * ***Animated Series for All Ages:***
    * ***Avatar: The Last Airbender:** A model for world-spanning quests, elemental magic, and balancing serious themes with humor.*
    * ***Steven Universe:** A reference for themes of empathy and non-violent conflict resolution.*
    * ***Adventure Time:** Inspiration for "weird fantasy" and a lighthearted, creative style of play.*
    * ***Kipo and the Age of Wonderbeasts:** An example of focusing on exploration and building friendships with strange creatures.*
  * *General genre studies including Sci-Fi, Mystery, Heist, and Action movies.*
* ***Cosmic Horror Literature***
  * *Works by authors like **H.P. Lovecraft** and **China Miéville**.*

## Tabletop & Gaming Sources

* ***Mainstream & Classic TTRPGs***
  * ***Dungeons & Dragons / Pathfinder:** The foundational TTRPGs, essential for player expectations.*
  * ***World of Darkness Games** (e.g., Vampire: The Masquerade, Mage: The Ascension): Excellent examples of games focused on personal horror and political intrigue.*
  * ***Ars Magica:** A key reference for detailed magic systems and long-term projects.*
  * ***Exalted:** A source for high-powered, over-the-top heroic action.*
* ***Indie & Story-Focused TTRPGs***
  * ***Blades in the Dark:** The modern standard for downtime mechanics, faction-based play, and "fail forward" design.*
  * ***Burning Wheel:** A masterclass in belief-driven roleplaying and detailed social "combat" systems.*
  * ***Fate:** A key inspiration for its elegant, flexible, and simple rules.*
  * ***Ironsworn:** A fantastic source for player-driven quests and progress-tracking mechanics.*
  * ***King Arthur Pendragon:** A classic game focused on generational play, glory, and character passions.*
  * ***Monsterhearts** (and other PbtA games): Key references for handling social leverage and narrative-focused mechanics.*
  * ***Numenera:** An inspiration for its three-stat system and ideas around resource expenditure.*
* ***Kid-Focused TTRPGs***
  * ***No Thank You, Evil\!:** A game designed for families with scalable rules.*
  * ***Hero Kids:** An introductory RPG with a simple framework and many published adventures.*
  * ***Amazing Tales:** A narrative-focused game with minimal mechanics to empower creativity.*
* ***OSR (Old School Revival) TTRPGs***
  * *General concepts like **Carousing Tables** and **Stronghold Building** rules.*
* ***Board Games & Card Games***
  * ***Arkham Horror: The Card Game:** A strong example of narrative-driven card play and resource management.*
  * ***Dune:** A benchmark for mechanics based on alliance, betrayal, and information warfare.*
  * ***Gloomhaven:** A direct inspiration for card-based fatigue and action-selection mechanics.*
  * ***Magic: The Gathering:** An inspiration for its simple core rules that lead to deep, emergent complexity through card interactions.*

## Video Game Sources

* ***CRPGs (Computer Role-Playing Games)***
  * ***The Witcher Series:** A key source for methodical monster hunting and mature narrative themes.*
  * ***The Soulsborne Series & Nioh Series:** Inspiration for gritty, challenging, and technical combat systems and environmental storytelling.*
  * ***Final Fantasy Series:** A benchmark for character class ("Job") systems, epic magical abilities, and campaign-spanning storylines.*
  * ***Crusader Kings:** A deep well of ideas for intrigue, dynastic politics, and emergent narrative events.*
  * ***Divinity: Original Sin 2:** A masterclass in systems-driven gameplay and rewarding player creativity in problem-solving.*
  * ***Disco Elysium:** An essential reference for non-combat gameplay, making intellectual and psychological stats into active, engaging mechanics.*
* ***Immersive Sims***
  * ***Thief: The Dark Project:** The benchmark for stealth-based gameplay, using light and sound as core mechanics.*
  * ***Dishonored Series:** A modern evolution of the genre, blending stealth with a versatile toolkit of supernatural powers in sandbox-style levels.*
* ***Strategy & Deck-building Games***
  * ***Slay the Spire:** A perfect source for elegant card design, keyword synergy, and creating difficult choices.* 
  * ***Darkest Dungeon:** A direct inspiration for stress, affliction, and "downward spiral" mechanics.*
  * ***Library of Ruina:** A great source for achieving ludonarrative harmony, where game mechanics and character identity are deeply intertwined.*
* ***Action/Adventure Games***
  * ***Red Dead Redemption 2:** An excellent model for "living world" downtime and character relationship-building activities.* 

# Document Purpose

This is a designer-facing document intended for internal use. 

It serves as a curated library of "places to look" when brainstorming the kinds of stories, play styles, and mechanical concepts we want to support. By providing a shared pool of inspirational touchstones—including books, films, and other games—this document helps to establish a common creative vocabulary for the design team. It is a source of ideas, not a book of rules.
