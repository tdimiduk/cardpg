# Research Mandate: The Engineering and Physics of Armor Protection

## 1. Core Objective

To acquire a detailed, quantitative understanding of how historical armor systems protect the human body against kinetic energy weapons. The goal is to move beyond abstract gaming abstractions of "AC" or "Damage Reduction" and understand the specific physical mechanics of protection: energy dissipation, force distribution, deformation, and material failure modes. We need to understand *how* armor works to model it correctly.

## 2. Core Research Directives

All findings must adhere to the following two directives:

*   **Directive 1: Quantitative Physics.** Prioritize hard data. We want values in Joules, Newtons, Pascals, and millimeters. How much energy does a gambeson absorb? What is the tensile strength of riveted mail rings? What force is required to dent 1.5mm mild steel breastplate?
*   **Directive 2: Mechanical Failure & Interaction.** Research must focus on the interaction between the weapon and the protection. How does a bodkin point interact with mail rings compared to a broadhead? How does blunt force transmit through rigid plate to the soft tissue underneath? Identify the specific conditions under which armor fails to protect.
*   **Directive 3: Source Criticality & Variable Analysis.** Leverage advanced reasoning to evaluate source quality. Prioritize modern engineering papers, ballistics testing, and high-fidelity experimental archaeology (e.g., correct metallurgy) over general historical assertions. When data conflicts, analyze the *variables* (e.g., steel quality, angle of incidence) that explain the discrepancy rather than averaging the results.

## 3. Assumed Context

*   **Technology Level:** Late Medieval / Early Renaissance (c. 1350-1550). Focus on the "High Era" of armor: tailored Gambesons, Riveted Mail, Brigandine/Coat of Plates, and articulated Plate Harness.
*   **Materials:** Mild steel (or wrought iron with variable carbon content), linen, wool, leather.
*   **Physics:** Real-world classical mechanics.

## 4. Key Research Areas (KRAs)

Structure the research to answer these key questions.

### KRA-1: Mechanics of Protection by Armor Type

Understanding the specific protective mechanism of each layer.

*   **1.1 Textile Armor (Gambeson/Aketon/Jack):**
    *   **Mechanism:** How does 15-30 layers of linen dissipate energy? Is it primarily spreading impact or resisting cut?
    *   **Limits:** What is the threshold for a sharp edge to slice through? How effectively does it dampen blunt force trauma (bruising/fracture) on its own?
*   **1.2 Flexible Metal (Riveted Mail):**
    *   **Mechanism:** How does mail prevent laceration? How do riveted rings fail (bursting rivet vs wire shear)?
    *   **Vulnerability:** The "Needle" problem: How much force is needed for a thin bodkin to burst a ring? The "Blunt" problem: How much energy is transferred directly to the wearer vs dissipated by ring collapse?
*   **1.3 Semi-Rigid/Composite (Brigandine/Coat of Plates):**
    *   **Mechanism:** How does the overlap of small plates disperse energy compared to a solid large plate?
    *   **Mobility vs Protection:** Does the flexibility introduce weak points (gaps between plates during movement)?
*   **1.4 Rigid Metal (Plate Harness):**
    *   **Mechanism:** Deflection (glancing blows) vs Absorption (deformation).
    *   **Deformation:** How much energy is required to dent a breastplate deep enough to cause injury (blunt trauma transmission)?
    *   **Penetration:** Is it physically possible for a sword slash to cut plate? (Verify this is impossible). What energy density (J/mm²) is required to pierce plate?

### KRA-2: Interaction with Damage Vectors

How protection varies by attack type.

*   **2.1 Piercing (Thrusts, Arrows, Picks):**
    *   **Energy Density:** Compare the penetration mechanics of high-velocity low-mass (arrow) vs low-velocity high-mass (spear/estoc).
    *   **Gap Seeking:** Documentation on the prevalence of "gap seeking" techniques in historical martial arts vs attempts to breach armor.
*   **2.2 Cutting/Shearing (Swords, Axes, Glaives):**
    *   **The Slide:** The mechanics of a blade sliding effectively vs biting.
    *   **Percussive Cut:** How an axe or large sword delivers blunt trauma even if it doesn't cut.
*   **2.3 Blunt Impact (Maces, Hammers):**
    *   **Concussion & Shock:** Transmission of force through rigid armor. Can you kill a man in plate without penetrating the armor? (Concussion, internal bleeding).
    *   **Deformation Lock:** Can armor be deformed enough to lock joints or crush limbs without breaching?

### KRA-3: Quantitative Comparatives

Data to help us set relative values.

*   **Relative Toughness:** If we set the "protection value" of a linen shirt to 0 and a full plate harness to 100, where do Gambeson, Mail, and Brigandine sit on that linear scale based on energy absorption/deflection capabilities?


### KRA-4: Contextual Effectiveness (Variable Analysis)

Gemini 3 specific: Analyze complex conditional success states.

*   **Angle of Incidence:** How does the effectiveness of plate armor change as the angle of attack moves from 90° (perpendicular) to 45°? (Glancing blow mechanics).
*   **Material Quality Variance:** How different is "Munition Grade" iron plate armor vs "Knightly" hardened steel armor? (e.g., 2mm iron vs 1.6mm tempered medium-carbon steel).
*   **The "Golden BB" / Weak Points:** What percentage of the body surface area remains vulnerable in a full harness? (Visor slits, armpits, palms). Quantify this exposure.

## 5. Desired Output Format

*   **Comparative Data Tables:** Columns for Armor Type, Resistance to Cut (Joules), Resistance to Pierce (Joules), Resistance to Blunt (Subjective/Qualitative).
*   **Myth-Busting Section:** Explicitly address common misconceptions (e.g., "Plate is clumsy/heavy," "Mail is easily pierced by everything").
*   **Physics Annotations:** Use formulas where helpful (e.g., KE = 1/2 mv²) to explain *why* a specific weapon is effective against specific armor.
