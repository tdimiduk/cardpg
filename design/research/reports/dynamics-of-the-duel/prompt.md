## Key Research Areas (KRAs)


Structure your research to investigate the moment-to-moment reality of a fight, distinct from the long-term medical consequences of wounds.


### KRA-1: The Tempo of Combat: Pacing, Duration, and Fatigue


* **1.1 The "Burst" of Action:**

    Determine the realistic duration of a single, explosive, all-out exchange of actions, citing physiological principles like the phosphagen energy system.

* **1.2 Engagement Duration:**

    Calculate how long a continuous, high-intensity confrontation can last before exhaustion degrades performance to incompetence. Estimate the number of "bursts" a trained combatant can sustain.

* **1.3 Combat Fatigue:**

    Detail the functional manifestations of acute, combat-related fatigue, specifically the degradation of precision, reaction time, defensive structure, and decision-making.

* **1.4 Adrenaline and Performance:**

    Analyze adrenaline's role in temporarily masking pain and fatigue, and detail the subsequent physiological "crash" once the immediate threat is over.


### KRA-2: The Anatomy of the Fight: Common Tactical Maneuvers


* **2.1 Seizing Initiative:**

    Analyze actions taken when not actively attacking, such as managing distance, probing defenses, and using feints to create openings.

* **2.2 Offensive Goals:**

    Analyze the tactical goals of offensive maneuvers beyond simple strikes, including binding, trapping, overwhelming a guard, and forcing disadvantageous positioning.

* **2.3 Defensive Tactics:**

    Provide a functional breakdown of defensive options (parrying, footwork, voiding, shield use), analyzing their tactical tradeoffs.

* **2.4 Grappling and Close-Quarters Combat:**

    Citing historical sources (`Ringen`), analyze the role, frequency, and primary objectives (disarms, throws, locks) of grappling in armed combat.


### KRA-3: The Cost of Engagement: Tactical Consequences Beyond Injury


* **3.1 Structural and Balance Disruption:**

    Analyze the mechanics of being knocked off-balance, staggered, or having posture broken. Detail the immediate functional impact and typical recovery time.

* **3.2 Positional Disadvantage:**

    Detail the tactical importance and common causes of being forced back, cornered, or maneuvered into environmental hazards.

* **3.3 Weapon and Shield Compromise:**

    Analyze the consequences of a weapon arm being deflected, a weapon being trapped or bound, a shield being beaten aside, or being disarmed.

* **3.4 Psychological Stress:**

    Analyze the functional impact of losing initiative, being forced onto the defensive, and the cognitive load of a chaotic, life-threatening situation.
