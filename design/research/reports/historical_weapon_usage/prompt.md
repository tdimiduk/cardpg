# Research Prompt: Historical Weapon Usage

## Objective
Analyze the physical, biomechanical, and contextual reasons for historical weapon selection across hunting, dueling, and warfare.
**Constraint:** Focus strictly on "Tangible Materiality." Prioritize quantitative data (joules, reach, weight, time-to-kill, armor interaction) over cultural symbolism or general narrative history.

## Key Research Areas (KRAs)

### 1. Hunting Contexts (Cross-Cultural)
*   **Target vs. Weapon:** Correlate specific prey animals (bodymass, hide thickness, speed) with weapon choice (bow draw weights, spear types, traps).
*   **Quantitative metrics:** Maximum effective range vs. typical engagement range. Time-to-die data for different shot placements/weapon types.
*   **Endurance vs. Burst:** Evidence of persistence hunting weaponry vs. ambush weaponry.
*   **Cultures:** Survey at least 5 distinct biomes/cultures (e.g., Inuit seal hunting, Maasai lion hunting, Medieval boar hunting, Indigenous Amazonian blowguns/bows, Steppe eagle/wolf hunting).

### 2. Dueling Contexts
*   **Armor Interaction:** How weapon choice shifts with the absence or presence of specific armor types (e.g., unarmored judicial duels vs. fully armored knightly combat).
*   **Lethality & Injury:** Physiological outcomes of specific dueling weapons (rapier puncture wounds vs. longsword cuts). Survival rates where data exists.
*   **Physics of Encounters:** Engagement distances, fight duration, and physical exertion levels.

### 3. Battlefield Contexts (Global Scope)
*   **Formation Density & Terrain:** How available space (e.g., shield wall vs. loose skirmish) dictates effective weapon length and swing arc. compare European Pike/Halberd formations with Japanese Yari ashigaru tactics or Zulu Impi close-quarters engagement.
*   **Fatigue Metrics:** Weights of standard battle kits and estimated combat endurance times across different climates.
*   **Armor Penetration & Counter-measures:**
    *   **European:** Kinetic energy to defeat mail/plate/gambeson (maces, warhammers, poleaxes).
    *   **East Asian:** Effectiveness of weapons (standing vs. mounted) against lamellar, lacquered leather, and brigandine. (e.g., Katana/Tachi vs. Mongol laminar).
    *   **Mesoamerican:** Mechanics of obsidian (macuahuitl) vs. cotton ichcahuipilli armor; cutting power vs. lack of metal armor.
    *   **Middle Eastern/South Asian:** Weapon adaptations for combating mail-and-plate mixtures (wootz steel cutting properties vs. blunt force).
*   **Material Availability & Climate:** How ecology limits armor choice (e.g., humidity ruling out heavy plate, lack of iron deposits) and how weaponry evolves to maximize lethality against the resulting protection levels.

### 4. Oddball/Niche Situations
*   **Improvised Weaponry:** Effectiveness and physics of agricultural tools used in rebellion (flails, scythes).
*   **Environment-Specific:** Weapons adapted for shifting footing (swamps, ship decks, mountaineering).

## Output Requirements
*   Provide tables comparing weapon stats (length, mass, center of percussion) where available.
*   Cite primary sources or archaeological reconstructions for physical data.
*   Avoid "honor" or "tradition" as primary explanations unless they translate to physical handicaps/advantages.
