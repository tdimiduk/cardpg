# Research Mandate: The Functional Impact of Pre-Modern Battlefield Injuries

## 1. Core Objective

To acquire a detailed, evidence-based understanding of the functional impact and progression of common battlefield injuries in a pre-antibiotic setting. The primary goal is to move beyond abstract medical classifications and gather tangible data on how specific injuries would affect a combatant's immediate capabilities, their chances of survival, and their long-term health if left untreated or minimally treated.

## 2. Core Research Directives

All findings must adhere to the following two directives:

* **Directive 1: Focus on Functional Impact.** Prioritize the practical, observable effects of an injury on a person's capabilities. For any given injury, the primary research question is: "What can this person *actually do*?" Detail the real-world constraints imposed by pain, shock, blood loss, and the mechanical failure of bodily structures.
* **Directive 2: Identify Cause-and-Effect for System Modeling.** The research must identify clear, cause-and-effect relationships between an injury and its consequences. The goal is to gather data suitable for modeling in a rules-based system. For example, research should clarify *why* and *how* a broken clavicle prevents the use of a shield, rather than just providing a medical definition of the fracture.

## 3. Assumed Context

* **Technology Level:** Pre-modern, roughly equivalent to the late medieval period. Assume the absence of antibiotics and modern surgical techniques.
* **Medical Knowledge:** Basic knowledge of wound dressing, cauterization, splinting, and amputation is present, but germ theory is unknown.

## 4. Key Research Areas (KRAs)

This research should be structured to answer the following key questions.

### KRA-1: The Immediate Functional Impact of Trauma (Seconds-to-Minutes Scale)

The goal is to understand what happens on the battlefield in the immediate aftermath of an injury.

* **1.1 Penetrating Trauma (Swords, Spears, Arrows):**
    * **Hemorrhage:** What is the realistic timeline for incapacitation from arterial bleeding (e.g., femoral, carotid) versus severe venous bleeding? At what points do weakness, disorientation, and loss of consciousness set in?
    * **Organ Damage:** What is the immediate functional impact of a punctured lung (pneumothorax)? Or a piercing wound to the abdomen (perforation of intestines, liver/spleen laceration)?
* **1.2 Blunt Force Trauma (Maces, Warhammers, Falls):**
    * **Skeletal Damage:** What is the functional consequence of a simple fracture to a limb (e.g., ulna, fibula) versus a compound fracture? How does a broken clavicle or rib cage immediately restrict movement and breathing?
    * **Neurological Trauma:** Beyond "knocked unconscious," what are the immediate symptoms of a severe concussion (e.g., from a helmeted head blow)? This includes disorientation, vertigo, blurred vision, and degraded motor control.
* **1.3 Pain & Shock:**
    * **Systemic Shock:** What are the physiological effects of acute physical trauma and pain on a person's ability to concentrate, make decisions, and perform complex actions?
    * **Psychological Trauma:** What are modern understandings of Acute Stress Reaction ("freezing" or panic in response to trauma), and how can they serve as an analogue for battlefield shock?

### KRA-2: The Progression of Untreated Wounds (Hours-to-Weeks Scale)

The goal is to chart the course of an injury after the immediate conflict has ended.

* **2.1 Infection:** What is the typical onset timeline for battlefield wound infections (gangrene, tetanus, sepsis)? What are the key stages and observable symptoms (fever, swelling, delirium)? What were the historical mortality rates for deep tissue wounds?
* **2.2 Improper Healing:** What are the long-term consequences of minimally-treated injuries? For example, the functional limitations of a poorly-set bone, a joint injury that develops arthritis, or nerve damage from a deep cut.
* **2.3 Internal Bleeding:** For blunt force trauma, what is the progression of internal bleeding from a ruptured organ? How long does a person typically remain functional before collapse?

### KRA-3: Modern Medical Processes as Models for Accelerated Healing

The goal is to research modern medical interventions to understand their discrete steps, goals, and risks. This information will serve as a realistic foundation for hypothetical healing systems.

* **3.1 Emergency Trauma Response (Triage & Stabilization):** Detail the process and objectives of modern battlefield trauma care. Focus on tools and techniques used to mitigate immediate threats without resolving the underlying injury. Examples include hemostatic agents (e.g., QuikClot), tourniquets, and chest seals.
* **3.2 Corrective Surgical Procedures:** Analyze the goals and risks of common surgical interventions for trauma. Examples include setting compound fractures, removing foreign objects from tissue, and repairing damaged organs. Detail the typical recovery times and potential for complications.
* **3.3 Medically Induced Coma for Recovery:** Explain the medical rationale and process for inducing a coma to facilitate healing from severe trauma. Describe what the body is doing during this state and the intended outcome.

## 5. Desired Output Format

The final report should present findings in a format that is easily digestible for game designers. Please prioritize:

* **Timelines:** For processes like blood loss and infection, provide clear timelines with functional benchmarks (e.g., "After 60 seconds of arterial bleeding, subject experiences severe dizziness...").
* **Functional Impact Tables:** For specific injuries, use tables to summarize the impact on various activities (e.g., Injury: Broken Ribs -> Impact on Lifting: Severe; Impact on Running: Moderate).
* **Case Studies:** Where possible, use historical accounts or modern medical case studies to provide concrete, narrative examples of injury progression and outcomes.
