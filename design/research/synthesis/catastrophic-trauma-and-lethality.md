# Factual Basis for Consequences: Catastrophic Trauma & Lethality

This document translates the findings of the "Quantitative Analysis of Physiological Timelines in Catastrophic Trauma" report into a practical reference for designing high-severity consequences. It provides an evidence-based framework for modeling the process of dying, reinforcing the core design precept that death is a telegraphed, narrative-rich process, not an instantaneous event.

## A Factual Basis for Tiered Lethality

The report establishes a clear hierarchy for the speed of death following different types of trauma, providing a model for conditions of varying urgency.

* **Hyper-Acute (seconds to < 5 minutes):** This timeframe is dominated by catastrophic vascular events.
    * An uncontained **Aortic or Cardiac Rupture** is fatal in **under one minute**.
    * A severed **Femoral Artery** is fatal in **2-5 minutes**.
    * This provides a factual basis for conditions representing the most urgent, life-threatening states where death is imminent within minutes.

* **Rapid (minutes to hours):** This timeframe involves progressive collapse from contained but severe internal processes.
    * Internal bleeding from a high-grade **ruptured liver or spleen** can take **minutes to hours** to become fatal.
    * A **Tension Pneumothorax** causes circulatory collapse and death over a period of **minutes**.
    * This supports severe injuries that are life-threatening on a strategic, rather than immediate, timescale.

* **Delayed (hours to days):** This timeframe is governed by the evolution of secondary injury cascades.
    * Penetrating brain injuries and epidural hematomas can lead to death over a period of **hours to days** as secondary effects like swelling and intracranial pressure build.
    * This provides a model for conditions that are not immediately catastrophic but will become fatal if not addressed during Downtime.

## Different Mechanisms of Incapacitation

The report quantifies the timelines for different fatal events, providing a basis for modeling unique types of harm.

* **Asphyxiation vs. Exsanguination:** Death from **strangulation** is a vascular event that causes unconsciousness in approximately **6.8 seconds** and is fatal in about **2-3 minutes**. This is a distinct physiological process and timeline compared to bleeding out from a major limb wound (fatal in 2-5 minutes).
* **The "Lucid Interval":** An **epidural hematoma** can produce a "lucid interval" where a person with a fatal head injury appears normal for **minutes to hours** before a sudden, rapid neurological decline leading to death. This provides a strong factual basis for a condition that has a delayed, "time bomb" effect.

## A Refined Model for Environmental Dangers

The report clarifies the immediate versus delayed causes of death in severe burn cases.

* In a fire, the most immediate cause of death is often **asphyxia** from inhaling superheated gas and toxins, which is fatal in **minutes**.
* The systemic effects of the burns themselves, such as **burn shock**, are a slower process that leads to death over a period of **hours to days**.
* This supports modeling a single event like "being set on fire" as having two distinct types of harm: an immediate respiratory threat and a longer-term thermal injury.
