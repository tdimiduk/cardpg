# Factual Basis for Design: Combat Spacing & The Three Measures

This document translates our research on historical combat into a practical, designer-facing model for tactical spacing. It is intended to provide a grounded, evidence-based framework for adjudicating movement, positioning, and area control during `Crisis Time`, reinforcing the "Casual Realism" principle of the game.

## Core Concepts: The Three Measures

Our model of combat spacing is built on the historical principles of the German fencing tradition, which divides an engagement into three distinct distances, or "Measures" (_Mensa_). This framework moves beyond a simple "reach" number to create a dynamic model where distance dictates viable tactics.

- **_Zufechten_** **("Fighting Towards"):** This is the **Outer Range**, or the approach phase. In this measure, combatants are too far apart to strike with a single, committed action. At least one preparatory step is required to close the distance. This is the zone of maneuver, assessment, and preparation.

- **_Krieg_** **("War"):** This is the **Threatened Range**, the primary fighting distance. From this measure, a combatant can strike their opponent with a single committed action, such as a lunge or a passing step combined with an attack. The tactical goal is often to keep your opponent in _Zufechten_ while you are in your own _Krieg_.

- **_Ringen am Schwert_** **("Wrestling at the Sword"):** This is the **Grappling Range**. This measure is entered when combatants are too close for the effective use of a weapon's primary striking surfaces (point and edge). Combat transitions to grappling, pommel strikes, half-swording, and leverages using the weapon's hilt and shaft.

## A Lexicon of Reach

To apply this model, we need a quantitative understanding of a combatant's area of control. This is defined by two key terms derived from the "Functional Dynamics of Pre-Modern Combat" report:

- **Static Reach:** This is the immediate threat zone a combatant can command with minimal body movement. It is the sum of their arm's length (~0.65 m) and the effective length of their weapon. Actions within this zone are the fastest and least costly.
- **Dynamic Reach:** This represents the maximum threat distance achievable with a single, committed forward movement, such as a lunge or a passing step. A conservative combat lunge adds approximately 0.75 m to 1.0 m to a combatant's reach. This value is heavily influenced by their equipment, with heavier armor imposing a significant metabolic cost that makes such explosive movements more taxing and thus shorter.
