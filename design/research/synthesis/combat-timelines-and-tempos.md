# Factual Basis for Design: Combat Timelines & Tempos

This document is a consolidated synthesis of our research into the functional, evidence-based timescales of pre-modern combat. Its purpose is to provide a single, unified reference for designers and Gamemasters to ensure that the pacing of `Crisis Time`, the duration of actions, and the effects of fatigue are grounded in a consistent and realistic framework.

The data is presented in sections, moving from the broad tactical principles and physiological phases of an encounter down to the granular duration of individual actions.

---

## 1. The Core Principles of Engagement

The "Functional Dynamics of Pre-Modern Combat" report establishes that historical combat was not a chaotic exchange but a tactical interplay of core variables. A robust model of combat should be built on these foundational principles from the German school of fencing.

- **Measure (_Mensa_):** The critical concept of distance. Combat is functionally divided into three ranges:
  - **_Zufechten_** **("Fighting Towards"):** The approach phase, where combatants are too far to strike in a single action.
  - **_Krieg_** **("War"):** The primary fighting distance, where a combatant can strike with a single committed action (e.g., a lunge).
  - **_Ringen am Schwert_** **("Wrestling at the Sword"):** The grappling range, too close for effective cuts and thrusts.
- **Tempo (_Vor, Nach, Indes_):** The principle governing initiative and timing.
  - **_Vor_** **("Before"):** Seizing the initiative to attack, forcing the opponent to react.
  - **_Nach_** **("After"):** The reactive, defensive state.
  - **_Indes_** **("Instantly"):** The critical moment of turning a defense into an immediate counter-attack, seizing the _Vor_ from the _Nach_.

## 2. The Physiological Rhythm of Combat

This table outlines the broad phases of a combat encounter, dictated by the body's energy systems and stress responses. This governs the overall pacing and rhythm of a fight.

| Timescale         | Physiological Event               | Functional State                                                                                                                                                                                               | Real-World Tactical Analogue                                                                                                                          |
| :---------------- | :-------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **0-15 Seconds**  | **Full ATP-PCr System Dominance** | **The "Peak Power Window."** A combatant's absolute maximum power output, fueled by the synergy of adrenaline and the complete phosphagen energy system.                                                       | The "Peak Power Window" for seizing the initiative (_Vor_); the initial, decisive exchange of a duel.                                                 |
| **15-90 Seconds** | **Glycolytic System Takeover**    | **Onset of Muscular Fatigue.** The "burn" begins as lactic acid accumulates. Power output drops, and fine motor control, accuracy, and reaction time begin to degrade.                                         | The "grind" of a fight; performance degrades, forcing a collapse from distance fencing to close-quarters grappling (_Ringen am Schwert_).             |
| **2-5 Minutes**   | **Phosphagen System Recovery**    | **Aerobic Recovery.** During lulls in the fighting, the aerobic system works to replenish the explosive energy stores of the ATP-PCr system. Full recovery can take several minutes of low-intensity activity. | Physiologically mandatory recovery period; low-intensity maneuvering between explosive bursts to replenish energy.                                    |
| **Up to 1 Hour**  | **Post-Adrenaline Crash**         | **Physiological and Psychological Crash.** Once the threat is over, the survivor experiences a period of severe fatigue, weakness, cognitive fog, and emotional dysregulation.                                 | The "Adrenaline Crash" and onset of Combat Stress Reaction (CSR); a period of extreme physiological and psychological vulnerability for the survivor. |

## 3. Tactical Performance Degradation

This table quantifies how a combatant's effectiveness degrades as a fight progresses through the physiological phases outlined above. The reaction times are calculated by applying degradation percentages from our research to a baseline simple visual reaction time of ~200 milliseconds.

| Timescale         | Dominant Tactical Phase                  | Biomechanical State & Bladework                                                                                                                                            | Reaction Time (Approx.)    |
| :---------------- | :--------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------- |
| **0-15 Seconds**  | **The Initial Exchange (_Vor_)**         | **Peak Performance.** Actions are fast, precise, and powerful. Bladework is complex and controlled, focused on single-tempo "master cuts" (_Meisterhäue_).                 | **~200 ms** (Baseline)     |
| **15-90 Seconds** | **The Tactical Chess Match (The Bind)**  | **Onset of Degradation.** Accuracy and fine motor control begin to decline (Δ ~14.7%). Tactics shift to the bind (_Fühlen_, _Winden_) which relies more on tactile "feel". | **~230 ms** (+15%)         |
| **90+ Seconds**   | **The Collapse to Grappling (_Ringen_)** | **Severe Degradation.** Significant loss of power and coordination. Bladework becomes sloppy and inconsistent. Gross motor skills (pushing, pulling) dominate.             | **~250-280 ms** (+25-40%)  |
| **Post-Combat**   | **The Victor's Crash**                   | **Systemic Impairment.** Slowed reactions, profound physical fatigue, and cognitive fog ("brain fog") set in.                                                              | **Significantly Impaired** |
