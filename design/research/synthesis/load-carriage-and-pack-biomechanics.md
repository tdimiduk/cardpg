# Load Carriage, Rigging Biomechanics, and Transport Energetics

This synthesis establishes the empirical and biomechanical baseline for human and quadrupedal load carriage, suspension rigging (pack frames, hip belts, yokes, and hand-carried loads), and metabolic expenditure across load-to-body-mass ratios.

---

## 1. Core Findings from Empirical Research

### 1.1 Nonlinear Metabolic Scaling & the LCDA Model vs. Pandolf Underestimation

Empirical evaluation of dismounted load carriage demonstrates that metabolic power expenditure ($\dot{M}$, in $\text{W}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$) increases as a nonlinear power function of the carried load relative to body mass, rather than scaling as an equivalent addition of body mass ([Looney et al., 2022](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/looney-2022-modeling-metabolic-costs-backpacking/extracted.md)).

Historical predictive algorithms commonly utilized in ergonomics—specifically the Pandolf equation (Pandolf et al., 1977) and the Minimum Mechanics model (Ludlow & Weyand, 2017)—consistently underpredict the true metabolic cost of heavy military load carriage:

- **Minimum Mechanics Model Underprediction:** Assumes external load mass incurs an identical metabolic cost per kilogram as human body mass. Across military backpacking trials up to 66% body mass, this model yields a severe negative bias of $-1.06 \pm 1.12\text{ W}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ (concordance correlation coefficient $\text{CCC} = 0.664$) internally, and $-1.23 \pm 1.02\text{ W}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ ($\text{CCC} = 0.513$) across independent historical datasets.
- **Pandolf Equation Underprediction:** Systematically underestimates metabolic rates during heavy loading ($>30\%$ body mass) and slower walking speeds, exhibiting a negative bias of $-0.44 \pm 0.74\text{ W}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ ($\text{CCC} = 0.908$) internally and $-0.46 \pm 0.67\text{ W}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ ($\text{CCC} = 0.863$) externally.
- **LCDA Backpacking Equation:** Developed for the US Army Load Carriage Decision Aid on 30 healthy soldiers walking at $0.45\text{ to }1.97\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ with backpack loads of 0%, 22%, 44%, and 66% body mass ($77 \pm 15\text{ kg}$ mean body mass). The formula models the nonlinear load effect via a fractional power function ($L_{\text{Bp}}^{1.36}$):
  $$\dot{M} = \dot{M}_{\text{Rest}} + \left(a + b S^{1/c} + d S^4\right) \cdot \left(1 + 1.96 \cdot L_{\text{Bp}}^{1.36}\right)$$
  where $S$ is walking speed ($\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$), $L_{\text{Bp}}$ is the load-to-body-mass ratio ($\text{load mass} / \text{body mass}$), and $\dot{M}_{\text{Rest}}$ is resting metabolic rate. The term $1.96 \cdot L_{\text{Bp}}^{1.36}$ dictates that light loads incur minor proportional cost increases, whereas heavy loads ($>40\%$ body mass) generate disproportionate, compounding energetic penalties.

Furthermore, maximum aerobic speed drops precipitously as backpack load increases:

- $0\%\text{ Body Mass (BM)}: 1.96 \pm 0.04\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ ($7.06\text{ km/h}$)
- $22\%\text{ BM}: 1.86 \pm 0.12\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ ($6.70\text{ km/h}$)
- $44\%\text{ BM}: 1.67 \pm 0.16\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ ($6.01\text{ km/h}$)
- $66\%\text{ BM}: 1.48 \pm 0.12\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ ($5.33\text{ km/h}$)

When loads exceed $75\%\text{ BM}$, human gait undergoes a catastrophic threshold transition, breaking normal pendular kinematics and demanding unsustainable motor unit recruitment ([Looney et al., 2022](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/looney-2022-modeling-metabolic-costs-backpacking/extracted.md); Harman et al., 2000).

### 1.2 Lean Body Mass as the Primary Physiological Anchor

Metabolic power requirements and physical load carriage capacity are anchored directly to Lean Body Mass (Fat-Free Mass, FFM), rather than total gross weight ([Looney et al., 2022](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/looney-2022-modeling-metabolic-costs-backpacking/extracted.md)):

- Baseline resting metabolic rate is calculated via Cunningham's equation ($RMR = 500 + 22 \times \text{LBM}$), where metabolically active tissue dictates basal oxygen uptake.
- Normalizing walking metabolic cost to lean body mass eliminates previously reported apparent sex differences between male and female warfighters carrying external loads (Silder et al., 2013). Adipose tissue represents an internal deadweight load that increases ground reaction forces without contributing muscular force generation or mitochondrial respiration capacity.

### 1.3 Load Suspension, Pelvic Transfer, and Tissue Pressure Tolerance

Backpack suspension architecture dictates the mechanical routing of gravitational loads through the skeleton ([Oberhofer et al., 2018](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/oberhofer-2018-backpack-weight-hip-belt-biomechanics/extracted.md)):

- **Force Transfer to the Pelvis:** A properly tensioned waist/hip belt backed by an internal or external pack frame transfers $30\%\text{ to }50\%+$ of the total vertical force off the spine and shoulder girdle directly onto the iliac crest (Lafiandra & Harman, 2004).
- **Tissue Pressure Thresholds:** Sensor-equipped biomechanical trials demonstrate that static peak contact pressure tolerance on the iliac crest is twice ($2.0\times$) as high as pressure on the shoulder girdle before reaching equivalent subjective discomfort or cutaneous ischemic pain thresholds (Wettenschwiler et al., 2015; Martin & Hooper, 2001).
- **Kinematic Stiffening under Load:** High backpack mass significantly restricts sagittal knee range of motion (ROM) ($59.9^\circ$ at $15\text{ kg}$ down to $56.7^\circ$ at $25\text{ kg}$, $p < 0.001$) and transverse pelvic rotational ROM ($6.2^\circ$ at $15\text{ kg}$ down to $4.7^\circ$ at $25\text{ kg}$, $p < 0.01$). This reduction in transverse pelvic rotation represents an active neuromuscular stabilization strategy to suppress torque generation around the longitudinal axis caused by pack inertia.
- **Dynamic Joint Loading:** Vertical peak ground reaction force (GRF) rises from $142.6\%\text{ BW}$ ($15\text{ kg}$) to $157.1\%\text{ BW}$ ($25\text{ kg}$), while peak knee sagittal joint moments increase from $11.3\text{ to }13.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$. Tight hip belt tension ($120\text{ N}$ vs. $30\text{ N}$) stabilizes the pack center of mass (CoM) relative to the torso CoM, reducing relative shear motion and mitigating lumbar spine shear stress ([Oberhofer et al., 2018](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/oberhofer-2018-backpack-weight-hip-belt-biomechanics/extracted.md)).

### 1.4 Carrying Geometry, Moment Arms, and Lumbar Spine Biomechanics

The geometry of load placement exerts a dramatic influence on lumbar disc compression and muscle recruitment ([Liu et al., 2023](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/liu-2023-bag-carrying-styles-lumbar-biomechanics/extracted.md)):

- **Intervertebral Disc Compression Exceeds Load Weight:** In all carrying styles, the increase in lumbar Intervertebral Compressive Force (ICF) substantially exceeds the actual gravitational mass of the bag, because paraspinal muscles must exert massive internal counter-moments to maintain spinal equilibrium.
- **Backpack (Symmetrical Clavicular/Thoracic):** Peak von Mises stress on the nucleus pulposus increases strictly **linearly** with carried mass across all lumbar motion segments (L1–L5) ($R^2 > 0.95$). At $20\%\text{ BW}$ ($14\text{ kg}$ for a $70\text{ kg}$ human), peak disc stress increases by $+64.6\%$, ICF at T12/L1 rises by $+255\text{ N}$, and L5/S1 intervertebral shear force (ISF) reaches $148\text{ N}$. Bilateral muscle activation remains balanced between left and right paraspinal groups.
- **Shoulder Bag (Unilateral Clavicular):** Induces lateral trunk tilt. At $20\%\text{ BW}$, peak disc stress rises by $+98.8\%$, ICF at T12/L1 increases by $+317\text{ N}$, and L5/S1 shear reaches $183\text{ N}$. Disc stress is linear at L1–L4, but transitions to **exponential** growth at the lower lumbar base (L4–L5: $y = 0.191 + 0.035 e^{0.131x}$, $R^2 > 0.95$). Ipsilateral psoas major and contralateral longissimus/multifidus experience persistent asymmetric contraction.
- **Handbag / One-Handed Carry (Unilateral Distal Hand):** Imposes an extreme lever arm. Contralateral paraspinal muscles (iliocostalis, longissimus, latissimus dorsi) contract vigorously like a crane counterweight to prevent ipsilateral collapse. Consequently, peak disc stress increases **exponentially** across **all lumbar segments** ($R^2 = 0.998$; L4–L5: $y = 0.212 + 0.018 e^{0.209x}$). At $20\%\text{ BW}$, peak nucleus pulposus stress spikes by $+164.7\%$, T12/L1 ICF increases by $+539\text{ N}$ ($>3.9\times$ the bag's actual weight in vertical force), and L5/S1 ISF spikes to $291\text{ N}$ ([Liu et al., 2023](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/liu-2023-bag-carrying-styles-lumbar-biomechanics/extracted.md)).

### 1.5 Distal Limb Load Multipliers and Extremity Encumbrance

The biomechanical location of carried mass governs energetic consumption via pendulum and lever mechanics ([US Army / Hasselquist et al., 2008](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/us-army-2008-body-armor-effects-ada504354/extracted.txt)):

- **Hand Mass Penalty:** Walking with a given mass on the hands incurs an energy cost $1.4\text{ to }1.9\times$ greater than bearing the identical mass centered on the torso (Soule & Goldman, 1969). Upper extremity weighting incurs a $13\%$ increase in metabolic expenditure per $1.0\text{ kg}$ added to the hands (Miller & Stamford, 1987).
- **Footwear and Ankle Mass Penalty:** Accelerating mass at the distal end of the lower limb pendulum is exceptionally costly: mass added to the feet incurs a metabolic expenditure $4.0\text{ to }6.0\times$ greater than an equivalent mass borne on the torso (Catlin & Dressendorfer, 1979; Holewijn et al., 1992; Jones et al., 1986; Soule & Goldman, 1969). Every $1.0\text{ kg}$ added to the boots/feet increases overall walking energy expenditure by $5\%\text{ to }10\%$.
- **Extremity Armor Encumbrance:** Adding $5.6\text{ to }6.4\text{ kg}$ of protective ballistic material to the limbs (arms and legs) increases walking $\dot{V}\text{O}_2$ scaled to body mass by $+22\%\text{ to }+26\%$ at $1.34\text{ m}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$ compared to wearing an armor vest alone, despite equivalent total torso loads. Kinetic braking forces at heel strike increase from $-1.86\text{ N}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ (unarmored) to $-2.14\text{ N}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ (vest) and $-2.40\text{ N}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ (vest + extremity armor). Stance time lengthens, swing time shortens, and stride width broadens, severely degrading maximal rush agility and lifting power ([US Army / Hasselquist et al., 2008](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/us-army-2008-body-armor-effects-ada504354/extracted.txt)).

### 1.6 Historical Military Load Benchmarks: The Roman _Sarcina_

Logistical records and experimental archaeology confirm that the human biped can transport formidable operational loads over sustained marches if rigged to distribute mass along the skeletal axis ([Roth, 1999](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/roth-1999-logistics-roman-army/extracted.txt)):

- **Fighting Load (_Expediti_):** Basic armor (chain mail _lorica hamata_ or segmented plate _lorica segmentata_), weapons (_gladius_, _pugio_, _pilum_), shield (_scutum_), and clothing totaled $18\text{ to }21\text{ kg}$ (Fuentes, 1991; Stolle, 1914), approximately $27\%\text{ to }32\%\text{ BM}$ for an ancient soldier ($170\text{ cm}$, $65.7\text{ kg}$).
- **Marching Pack (_Sarcina_ / "Marian Mules"):** In addition to fighting armor and weapons, legionaries carried personal gear, cooking vessels (_vasa_), entrenching tools (_dolabra_, sickle, basket), and up to 14–17 days of unground grain (_frumentum_, $850\text{ g/day}$ or 1 modius/day per 8-man _contubernium_). Vegetius (_Epit._ 1.19) documents a regulation marching load of 60 Roman pounds ($19.6\text{ to }20.0\text{ kg}$) exclusive of armor, driving total carried mass to $40\text{ to }45\text{ kg}$ ($60\%\text{ to }68\%\text{ BM}$).
- **The Marian _Furca_ Rig:** This load was made portable without modern hip harnesses through the Marian carrying pole (_furca_) with a transverse crossbar (_tabella_). The pack sat on the crossbar and rested balanced against the top rim of the _scutum_ slung over the soldier's back. This mechanical arrangement transferred the vertical load down the rigid curve of the shield into the lumbar/pelvic region, functioning as an external frame. Furthermore, it permitted instant load jettisoning: releasing the _furca_ dropped the $20\text{ kg}$ baggage in 2 seconds, freeing the soldier to fight unencumbered as an _expeditus_.
- **Alpine Field Verification:** Marcus Junkelmann's experimental march over the Alps (1986) demonstrated that ordinary civilians carrying $43\text{ to }46\text{ kg}$ of reconstructed Roman legionary gear covered $500\text{ km}$ at an average pace of $25\text{ km/day}$, validating that high absolute loads are humanly sustainable if mechanically rigged against the back and supported by adequate caloric intake ($\approx 3,000\text{ kcal/day}$) ([Roth, 1999](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/roth-1999-logistics-roman-army/extracted.txt)).

### 1.7 Equid Carrying Capacities and Physiological Thresholds

Quadrupedal pack transport is governed by distinct metabolic and skeletal failure boundaries ([Bukhari et al., 2021](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/bukhari-2021-mounted-load-carrying-equids-review/extracted.md)):

- **Horse (_Equus caballus_) Aerobic Ceiling (20% BWR):** Horses operate aerobically and maintain stable physiological parameters up to a Body Weight Ratio (BWR) of approximately $20\%\text{ to }22.7\%$ (typically $80\text{ to }90\text{ kg}$ for a $450\text{ kg}$ mount). At loads $\le 20\%\text{ BWR}$, plasma lactate remains low ($<2.0\text{ mmol/L}$), stride kinematics remain symmetric, and serum creatine kinase (CK) exhibits no pathological elevation.
- **Horse Strain & Microtrauma Threshold (25%–30% BWR):** Elevating mounted load to $25\%\text{ to }30\%\text{ BWR}$ triggers a sharp physiological penalty. Heart rate increases by $+54\text{ to }+59\text{ bpm}$; respiration rate increases from $39\text{ to }86\text{ breaths/min}$; and plasma lactate spikes exponentially from $3.6\text{ to }7.9\text{ mmol/L}$ (requiring 24 to 48 hours to clear back to baseline $0.9\text{ mmol/L}$). Serum CK activities spike significantly immediately and 24–48 hours post-exercise, indicating acute skeletal muscle damage and rhabdomyolysis microtrauma. Trot symmetry deteriorates, producing measurable lameness at $>27.5\%\text{ BWR}$ (Dyson et al., 2020).
- **Skeletal Determinants:** The circumference of the third metacarpal bone ("cannon bone") and the transverse width of the loin are the primary structural determinants of equine load capacity. Horses loaded heavily without proportional cannon bone circumference suffer structural biomechanical collapse (Garlinghouse et al., 1999).
- **Donkey (_Equus asinus_) Superior Structural Capacity (40%–50% BWR):** Donkeys possess unique anatomical adaptations: straight backs with minimal dorsal flexion, low withers, upright hooves with steep dorsal angles, and a $25\%$ thicker integument depth at the third phalanx. Indian working donkeys ($110\text{ to }142\text{ kg}$ body mass) sustainably carry $40\%\text{ to }50\%\text{ BWR}$ ($45\text{ to }70\text{ kg}$) over 6-hour work bouts with modest pulse increases ($+18\text{ to }+21\text{ bpm}$) and stable lactate levels ($1.03\text{ to }1.20\text{ mmol/L}$) (Legha et al., 2018).
- **Industrial Overload in Brick Kilns:** In unregulated commercial brick kilns, working donkeys are routinely loaded with $100\text{ to }150\text{ kg}$ ($80\%\text{ to }110\%+\text{ BWR}$), resulting in frequent collapse, deep open saddle ulcerations, spinal fractures, tendon ruptures, and severe mortality ([Bukhari et al., 2021](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/bukhari-2021-mounted-load-carrying-equids-review/extracted.md); [Roth, 1999](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/roth-1999-logistics-roman-army/extracted.txt)).

### 1.8 Inter-Individual Variance vs. Training Adaptations in Load Carriage

A critical question for exercise physiology and game systems design is whether physical training allows humans to "carry heavy loads more efficiently" or whether carrying capacity is structurally bounded by baseline anatomy:

- **Invariance of Walking Mechanical Efficiency (~0–3% Change):** Human walking kinematics operate as an inverted pendulum where potential and kinetic energy exchange with $60\%\text{–}70\%$ mechanical efficiency. Moving a given external payload (whether a backpack or steel armor) requires virtually identical metabolic oxygen consumption per step ($\sim 0.2\text{ mL O}_2/\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}$) in untrained novices and elite conditioned warfighters (Jones et al., 1984; [Looney et al., 2022](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/looney-2022-modeling-metabolic-costs-backpacking/extracted.md)). Training does _not_ alter gravitational physics or biomechanical leverage—a $30\text{ kg}$ load costs the exact same mechanical work and oxygen volume regardless of veteran status.
- **Inter-Individual Variance (Driven by Lean Body Mass & Absolute $\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$):** What distinguishes individuals is not walking economy, but the size of their physiological "engine." Across healthy adult humans, Lean Body Mass (LBM) varies by $\sim 2.0\times$ (e.g., $36\text{ kg}$ LBM in a slight $52\text{ kg}$ adult vs. $74\text{ kg}$ LBM in a muscular $85\text{ kg}$ strength-endurance athlete) and absolute aerobic capacity ($\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$ in $\text{L/min}$) varies by $\sim 2.5\times$ ($1.85\text{ vs. }4.80\text{ L/min}$; Lyons et al., 2005; Bilzon et al., 2001). This anatomical divergence establishes a natural $\sim 1.5\times\text{ to }2.0\times$ span in baseline sustainable load increments:
  - _Slight Adult:_ $\approx 12\text{ kg}$ per aerobic increment ($15\%\text{–}20\%\text{ LBM}$).
  - _Standard Fit Adult:_ $\approx 15\text{ kg}$ per aerobic increment.
  - _Brawny / Heavy Athlete:_ $\approx 18\text{–}20\text{ kg}$ per aerobic increment.
- **Intra-Individual Training Adaptations (Plateauing Ceiling):** Rigorous longitudinal military studies examining concurrent physical conditioning (progressive road marching with rucksacks + heavy compound resistance training + aerobic intervals over 8 to 24 weeks; Kraemer et al., 2001, 2004; Harman et al., 2008; Knapik et al., 2012) prove that intra-individual adaptations are strictly bounded:
  - Adult LBM increases by only $+2\%\text{ to }+5\%$ ($+1.5\text{ to }3.5\text{ kg}$ of functional muscle mass).
  - Absolute $\dot{V}\text{O}_{2\max}$ increases by $+10\%\text{ to }+20\%$.
  - These systemic gains yield an aggregate $+15\%\text{ to }+25\%$ improvement in sustainable loaded march capacity. Crucially, this gain represents a one-time adaptation taking an unconditioned novice into optimal "trail shape," after which human skeletal, vertebral, and cardiopulmonary load-carriage performance plateaus. Veteran status does not continuously multiply skeletal load capacity.

## 3. Clinical Pathology & Material Failure Modes

### 3.1 Neuropathies & Peripheral Nerve Compression

1. **Rucksack Palsy (Brachial Plexopathy):** Continuous traction and direct clavicular compression by pack shoulder straps on the upper trunk of the brachial plexus (C5–C6) causes local ischemia and axonal neuropraxia. Manifests clinically as motor weakness in the deltoid, biceps brachii, and brachioradialis, accompanied by sensory numbness along the lateral forearm and hand. Eliminating rucksack palsy requires transferring $\ge 30\%$ of pack force to the iliac crest via a rigid waist harness ([Oberhofer et al., 2018](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/oberhofer-2018-backpack-weight-hip-belt-biomechanics/extracted.md)).
2. **Meralgia Paresthetica:** Excessive hip belt compression or improper placement over the anterior superior iliac spine (ASIS) entraps the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve (L2–L3), causing burning paresthesia and numbness over the anterolateral thigh.

### 3.2 Lumbar Spine & Disc Degradation

1. **Nucleus Pulposus Extrusion:** Axial compressive forces ($>3,000\text{ N}$) coupled with anterior trunk flexion force the nucleus pulposus posteriorly against the annulus fibrosus ([Liu et al., 2023](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/liu-2023-bag-carrying-styles-lumbar-biomechanics/extracted.md)). Asymmetric unilateral loads (handbags, unrigged sacks) induce exponential increases in peak shear stress at L4–L5 and L5–S1, vastly accelerating degenerative disc herniation.
2. **Pars Interarticularis Fatigue:** Repetitive ground reaction impacts under heavy load ($>150\%\text{ BW}$) concentrate high tensile stresses on the vertebral pars interarticularis, precipitating bilateral stress fractures (spondylolysis) and forward vertebral slippage (spondylolisthesis).

### 3.3 Integumentary & Tendinous Breakdown

1. **Frictional Shear Blisters:** Foot-ground shear stress compounds under carried load as heel-strike and toe-off forces rise from $11.4\text{ to }13.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}$ ([US Army / Hasselquist et al., 2008](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/us-army-2008-body-armor-effects-ada504354/extracted.txt)). Hydration of stratum corneum combined with cyclic intra-epidermal shear produces cleavage within the stratum spinosum, forming fluid-filled friction blisters.
2. **Equid Pressure Ulcers & Spondylopathy:** Unpadded pack-saddles under loads $>30\%\text{ BWR}$ generate capillary-closing pressures ($>4\text{ kPa}$) over the dorsal withers of horses and donkeys, producing localized cutaneous necrosis, purulent back sores, and supraspinous ligamentous bursitis ("fistulous withers") ([Bukhari et al., 2021](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/bukhari-2021-mounted-load-carrying-equids-review/extracted.md)).
