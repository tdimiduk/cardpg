# Factual Basis for Consequences: Physical Harm & Trauma

This document translates the findings of our research into a practical reference for designing consequences related to physical injury, combat, and bodily duress. It is organized by the type and timeline of harm, providing quantified functional impacts and mapping specific real-world conditions to the game's four severity levels.

## Core Design Insights from Research

- **Cognitive Failure Precedes Physical Collapse:** In almost all analyzed scenarios—hemorrhage, hypothermia, starvation, exhaustion—a person's judgment, decision-making, and situational awareness are severely compromised _before_ they are physically unable to act. A combatant in Class III hemorrhagic shock becomes confused and disoriented long before they lose consciousness. Similarly, the "apathy trap" of mild hypothermia degrades the motivation for self-rescue long before the body shuts down. This is a critical insight for consequence design, suggesting that higher-severity consequences should inflict cognitive penalties (e.g., restricting card draw, forcing discards) in addition to physical ones.
- **The Multiplier Effect:** The reports emphasize that stressors are synergistic, not additive. An exhausted individual is more vulnerable to dehydration, which in turn makes them more susceptible to cold. This supports a design where having multiple, lower-severity conditions can be just as dangerous as a single high-severity one, and where mechanics could model this synergy directly.
- **Infection as a "Death Timer":** In a pre-antibiotic setting, any open wound is a countdown to a likely fatal infection. Conditions like a **Compound Fracture** or a **Gut Wound** are not just injuries; they are the start of a process that, without extraordinary (perhaps magical) intervention, will lead to death. This provides a strong justification for some of the most severe, long-term consequences that create a desperate, time-sensitive objective for the party.

## Section 1: Acute Trauma (Crisis Time Consequences)

These conditions have an immediate or very rapid onset (seconds to minutes), making them suitable models for consequences suffered during **Crisis Time**. The "Degradation" scale rates functional impairment from **0 (no impairment)** to **10 (complete incapacitation)**.

### 1.1 Hemorrhagic Shock (Blood Loss)

This provides a direct and well-defined progression for "Bleeding" consequences. The four classes of shock map perfectly to our four severity levels.

| Severity | Medical State   | Blood Loss    | Onset               | Functional Degradation (0-10 Scale) | Key Effects & Narrative Cues                                                                                                   |
| :------- | :-------------- | :------------ | :------------------ | :---------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | Class I Shock   | <15% (<750ml) | Immediate           | 1/10.                               | Alert but anxious. No significant combat impairment. A "flesh wound."                                                          |
| 2        | Class II Shock  | 15-30%        | 1-5 mins            | 4/10.                               | Weakness, dizziness on exertion. Mild confusion. Fine motor skills degrade. Difficulty aiming, fumbling with items.            |
| 3        | Class III Shock | 30-40%        | 2-10 mins           | 8/10.                               | Severe confusion/disorientation. Cannot make tactical decisions. Severe weakness, stumbling gait, likely to fall. A liability. |
| 4        | Class IV Shock  | >40%          | 2-5 mins (arterial) | 10/10.                              | Unconscious or comatose. Incapacitated. Bleeding out.                                                                          |

### 1.2 Neurological Disruption (Blunt Head Trauma)

A severe concussion is not a simple "stun" but a complete crash of the brain's ability to process and act. These effects are immediate.

| Severity | Medical State         | Functional Degradation (0-10 Scale) | Key Effects & Narrative Cues                                                                                                                       |
| :------- | :-------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2        | Mild Concussion       | 5/10.                               | Dazed, slowed thinking, blurred vision. May "see stars". Actions are hindered, perception is unreliable.                                           |
| 3        | Severe Concussion     | 8/10.                               | Profound confusion, disorientation, amnesia. Loss of balance, unsteady gait (ataxia), slurred speech. Cannot effectively fight or follow commands. |
| 4        | Loss of Consciousness | 10/10.                              | Unconscious. Incapacitated.                                                                                                                        |

### 1.3 Skeletal & Structural Failure

These injuries represent immediate mechanical failure of the body, making certain actions physically impossible.

| Severity | Injury               | Functional Degradation (0-10 Scale) | Key Effects & Narrative Cues                                                                                                                         |
| :------- | :------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2        | Rib Fracture         | 4/10.                               | Sharp, localized pain. Taking deep breaths or performing strenuous actions is excruciating. Limits aerobic capacity.                                 |
| 3        | Clavicle Fracture    | 7/10.                               | The collarbone acts as a strut for the arm; its fracture makes it impossible to lift a shield, use a two-handed weapon, or draw a bow.               |
| 3        | Arm Fracture (Major) | 7/10.                               | The arm is rendered useless for load-bearing tasks. Cannot effectively wield a two-handed weapon, use a shield, or climb.                            |
| 4        | Leg Fracture (Major) | 9/10.                               | Total incapacitation of the limb. Cannot bear weight or move the limb and its adjacent joints. Non-ambulatory and effectively out of a mobile fight. |

### 1.4 Thoracic & Abdominal Wounds

These are insidious injuries where the immediate impact can be deceptive.

| Severity | Injury                   | Functional Degradation (0-10 Scale) | Key Effects & Narrative Cues                                                                                                                                                   |
| :------- | :----------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 2        | Open Pneumothorax        | 5/10.                               | "Sucking chest wound." Significant pain, shortness of breath, coughing blood. Can remain conscious and capable of limited action.                                              |
| 3        | Gut Wound (Hollow Organ) | 3/10 (progressing to 9/10).         | Deceptively mild initial pain. The true "damage" is the initiation of a fatal infection (peritonitis). A delayed-fuse death sentence.                                          |
| 4        | Tension Pneumothorax     | 10/10 within minutes.               | Air pressure builds in the chest, collapsing a lung and shifting the heart, leading to rapid cardiovascular collapse and death. This is a catastrophic, rapidly fatal outcome. |

## Section 2: Sustained Hardship & Progressive Decline

These conditions develop over hours, days, or weeks. They are suitable models for persistent consequences and emphasize the importance of rest, resources, and long-term care.

### 2.1 Deprivation (Water, Food, Sleep)

The effects of deprivation are synergistic, with cognitive failure preceding physical collapse.

| Severity | Condition                                           | Onset       | Functional Degradation (0-10 Scale) | Key Effects & Narrative Cues                                                                                                                                |
| :------- | :-------------------------------------------------- | :---------- | :---------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | Mild Dehydration (1-2% loss)                        | 6-12 hours  | 2/10.                               | Thirst, fatigue, irritability. Reaction time slows by 12-18%; attention and memory are impaired.                                                            |
| 2        | Moderate Dehydration (2-4% loss) / 24hrs No Sleep   | 12-24 hours | 5/10.                               | Physical performance declines by ~20%. Headache, dizziness. Cognitive work capacity declines by 25% after 24hrs of sleep loss. Severe attention deficits.   |
| 3        | Severe Dehydration (5-10% loss) / 48-72hrs No Sleep | 24-72 hours | 8/10.                               | Lethargy, confusion, rapid pulse. Complex task performance collapses to <10% of baseline after 48-72 hours of sleep loss. Catastrophic failure of judgment. |
| 4        | Starvation (Late Phase)                             | 2-4+ Weeks  | 9/10.                               | Near-total functional collapse. Severely impaired concentration, judgment, and comprehension. Emaciated and too weak for significant labor.                 |

### 2.2 Environmental Exposure (Hypothermia)

The danger of hypothermia is the "apathy trap"—the mind fails before the body, preventing self-rescue.

| Severity | Medical State        | Onset       | Functional Degradation (0-10 Scale) | Key Effects & Narrative Cues                                                                                                                         |
| :------- | :------------------- | :---------- | :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1        | Mild Hypothermia     | 30-60+ mins | 4/10.                               | Vigorous, uncontrollable shivering. Onset of confusion, lethargy, apathy. Fine motor control is lost (difficult to start a fire, tie knots).         |
| 2        | Moderate Hypothermia | 1-2+ hours  | 8/10.                               | Shivering ceases (an ominous sign). Muscles become rigid, speech is slurred, movements are slow and uncoordinated. Delirium and irrational behavior. |
| 3        | Severe Hypothermia   | 1-3+ hours  | 10/10.                              | Loss of consciousness. Pulse and respiration are nearly undetectable. Appears deceased.                                                              |

## Section 3: The "Simulationist" Wall & Healing Design

Certain outcomes in the research have such a grim prognosis in a pre-modern, pre-antibiotic setting that they represent a "wall" for casual realism. Handing out these consequences without providing a clear, albeit difficult, path to resolution (e.g., magical healing, advanced alchemy) could violate the "Fun" guiding principle.

**Conditions with Extremely Poor Prognosis:**

- **Compound (Open) Fracture:** Effectively a death sentence. The exposure of bone to the environment makes deep, untreatable infection (osteomyelitis) and sepsis a near-certainty. **Removal Condition:** Should likely require powerful, targeted "Knit Bone" and "Purify Wound" type effects.
- **Gut Wound / Peritonitis:** A perforation of the intestines initiates a fatal infection (peritonitis) that leads to sepsis and death within 2-4 days. **Removal Condition:** Requires a magical intervention capable of both repairing the internal organ and sterilizing the abdominal cavity.
- **Tetanus ("Lockjaw"):** Caused by _Clostridium tetani_ in deep wounds. After a 3-21 day incubation, it produces agonizing, full-body muscle spasms powerful enough to break bones, leading to death by asphyxiation. **Removal Condition:** This would require a specific "Cure Disease" or "Neutralize Toxin" effect, as simply healing the initial wound does nothing to stop the neurotoxin.
- **Gas Gangrene:** Caused by other _Clostridium_ species in deep, necrotic wounds. It spreads with terrifying speed (hours to days), liquefying muscle tissue and causing systemic shock and death. **Removal Condition:** This suggests a need for a "Purge Corruption" or "Regenerate Tissue" effect that can act extremely quickly to counteract the necrosis.
