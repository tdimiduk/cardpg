# Subterranean Gases and Blackdamp Toxicity

This synthesis provides physical, chemical, and physiological benchmarks for atmospheric hazards in ancient, unventilated subterranean structures, mines, and burial mounds.

---

## 1. Taxonomy and Physical Behavior of Subterranean "Damps"

Underground atmospheric hazards fall into distinct chemical and physical categories:

1. **Blackdamp (Chokedamp / Stythe):**
   - **Composition:** Mixture of nitrogen ($85-90\%$) and carbon dioxide ($10-15\%$) resulting from the slow oxidation of pyrites, organic detritus, and stagnant water, with oxygen depleted.
   - **Density:** Denser than atmospheric air (specific gravity $\approx 1.15 - 1.5$ relative to air).
   - **Behavior:** Pools in low-lying depressions, sumps, dead ends, and stairwells, establishing sharp horizontal stratification layers. Odorless and invisible.
2. **Firedamp:**
   - **Composition:** Primarily methane ($CH_4$), formed by anaerobic decomposition.
   - **Density:** Much lighter than air (specific gravity $\approx 0.55$).
   - **Behavior:** Rises immediately to ceiling vaults, dome arches, and roof cavities. Odorless and non-toxic in pure form; explosive in air between **5% and 15%**.
3. **Stinkdamp:**
   - **Composition:** Hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$), released from sulfurous groundwaters and decaying matter.
   - **Behavior:** Pungent rotten-egg stench at low concentrations; rapidly paralyzes olfactory nerves at $>100\ \text{ppm}$, lulling victims into a false sense of safety before causing lethal respiratory arrest.

## 2. Flame Extinction vs. Human Survival Margins

Historical mining experiments by J.S. Haldane established precise thresholds for combustion using candles and open flames:

- **Flame Sputtering:** Initiates when atmospheric oxygen falls below **$17.5\%\ O_2$**.
- **Flame Extinction:** Open torches and oil lamps are extinguished when oxygen falls below **$15.0\% - 16.5\%\ O_2$**.
- **Human Functional Window:** A healthy person resting or walking moderately remains fully conscious and functional in an atmosphere of $15\% - 16\%\ O_2$ (manifesting only slightly increased breathing depth).
- **The Early Warning Function:** An open flame carried at head height serves as a reliable early warning, going out before human incapacitation occurs, _provided the flame is held at the breathing elevation_.

## 3. Floor Stratification and the "Torch Height Trap"

Because $CO_2$ is 1.5 times denser than air, a stagnant crypt or sump with zero ventilation establishes a sharp stratification boundary:

- The bottom 0.5 to 1.0 meter can be filled with concentrated blackdamp ($<4\%\ O_2,\ >10\%\ CO_2$), while the air at 1.5 to 2.0 meters remains breathable ($>18\%\ O_2$).
- **The Trap:** An explorer wading upright with a torch at shoulder height observes a brightly burning flame. If they kneel to inspect an inscription, drop a tool, or bend down, their face dips beneath the stratification line.
- **Acute Hypoxic Syncope:** Inhaling pure blackdamp ($<6\%\ O_2$) triggers loss of consciousness within **15 to 30 seconds** (the circulation time of deoxygenated blood from lungs to brain). The victim collapses onto the floor into the deep gas pool and dies within 3 to 5 minutes unless hauled out immediately.

## 4. Methane Ignition Realities

- **Mud Disturbance:** Stepping into peat mud releases bubbles that produce only brief, harmless flame flickers if ignited at the surface (will-o'-the-wisp).
- **Ceiling Pocket Deflagration:** If methane accumulates in an unventilated dead-end ceiling arch within its explosive range (5–15%), raising a blazing torch into the pocket triggers a violent deflagrating flame roll across the ceiling, scorching hair and skin and consuming localized oxygen.
