# Literature Note: How OSR Teaches Us That Exploration Is Logistics

## 1. Source Overview

- **Source:** _The RPG Gazette_ (Sept 11, 2026)
- **Author:** The RPG Gazette editorial
- **Core Premise:** Responds to the pervasive modern critique that tracking torches, rations, encumbrance, and hireling morale is "boring wargaming bookkeeping." The essay argues that resource logistics is the primary structural mechanism by which exploration generates stakes, environmental indifference, and genuine player agency.

---

## 2. Key Theses & Mechanics from the Essay

1. **Logistics Transforms Time and Geography into Mechanical Pressure:**
   A dungeon or wilderness is dangerous not merely because of monsters (immediate danger), but because of the steady constriction of limited light, rations, and carrying capacity (long-term danger).
2. **Meaningful Decisions Require Meaningful Constraints:**
   The fundamental adventuring decision—_whether to turn back or push deeper_—has drama only when resources are dwindling. Without attrition, deciding to retreat is trivial or arbitrary.
3. **Danger is Progressive, Not Random:**
   Arbitrary danger kills a character on a single bad roll of the dice. Logistic danger kills a character through the compounding consequences of a sequence of compromised player choices.
4. **"The Town is Part of the Dungeon":**
   Logistics creates an economic and social breathing loop between town and dungeon. Hirelings gossip, town supplies dictate expedition endurance, and dungeon treasure fuels town outfitting.
5. **Encumbrance as Moral/Strategic Dilemma:**
   Treasure is not just a reward; carrying capacity turns treasure into an active logistics dilemma (caching, abandoning gear, multiple trips).

## 3. Comparative Analysis & Application to CardPG

The essay articulates the exact ludonarrative goals CardPG holds for exploration and attrition, but highlights a crucial tabletop ergonomics problem that CardPG is positioned to solve:

| OSR Convention                 | OSR Implementation                                                         | CardPG Implementation (Card Economy)                                                                                                                                             |
| :----------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pacing / Time Pressure**     | Tracking torch minutes, turns, and oil flasks on paper.                    | **Deck Dilution & Cycling:** Expending cards from hand/deck. Every turn in a hazard spends cards; the draw pile shrinks.                                                         |
| **Creeping Exhaustion**        | Deducting daily rations, water, encumbrance penalties.                     | **Status Cards in Deck:** Adding `Fatigue` and `Minor Wound` cards directly into the player's 24-card deck. Dilution degrades hand quality naturally without manual tally marks. |
| **Environmental Indifference** | Wandering monster tables, torches burning out in real time (_Shadowdark_). | **Consequence Decks & Clocks/Fronts:** The environment hits back via Consequence draws on actions, and campaign clocks tick while players rest or hesitate.                      |
| **Expedition Risk / Return**   | Weighing treasure coins against carrying capacity limits.                  | **Inventory Cards & Tradeoffs:** Equipping/carrying items consumes deck or slot space, creating tangible choices between survival tools and loot.                                |
| **Town & Downtime Loop**       | Restocking rations, paying retainers, resting in taverns.                  | **Downtime Pillar:** Transforming severe injuries to manageable burdens; purging Fatigue from decks; advancing World Fronts while the party recovers.                            |

### The "Bookkeeping" Distinction

The essay correctly defends the _structural necessity_ of logistics, but glosses over the physical table friction: writing down 10-minute dungeon turns, tracking torch burn times, and calculating coin weights can be cumbersome.

**CardPG's design thesis for Exploration:**

> We achieve the _exact emotional and structural pressure_ of OSR logistics—the creeping dread of dwindling reserves, the hard choice to turn back, and the indifference of the world—**without spreadsheet friction**.
>
> The physical artifact of the **card deck** acts as our supply chain, clock, and stamina bar all at once. When your deck fills with Fatigue, you feel the exhaustion in your hands without writing down a single number.

## 4. Actionable Next Steps for CardPG

1. **`consequences-logistics.md` & `consequences-exploration.md`:**
   Ensure logistics consequences directly interact with deck health and equipment slots (e.g., _Spoiled Rations_, _Lost Pack Lead_, _Frayed Straps_ forcing deck discards or tactical delays).
2. **Exploration Module Design:**
   Frame exploration hazards as **resource trade-off choices** (burn cards to secure a shortcut vs. take the safe, slow route that allows Clocks to advance) rather than binary pass/fail agility checks.
3. Think about equipment tracking card mechanics.
4. Think about encumberance mechanics that will play well at the table.
5. Think about "economy" decks for towns to let them ebb and flow (and react to things the players do)
