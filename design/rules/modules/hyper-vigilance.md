# Rules Module: Hyper-Vigilance

In the core rules, characters maintain **[[Vigilance]]** as a pool of 0 to 4 cards, representing disciplined, sustainable readiness on the road and on watch, paid roughly every in-game hour.

**Hyper-Vigilance** introduces an optional tactical dial that allows characters to push beyond standard limits to a pool of **5 or 6 cards**. In exchange for overwhelming opening reaction speed, this extreme alertness doubles the rate of mental and physical fatigue, cutting the payment interval to **every 30 minutes**.

---

## Player Rules

### The Concept: Pushing into Acute Threat Focus

Standard Vigilance (0 to 4 cards) reflects a seasoned traveler's sustainable awareness: weapons slung or loosely carried, senses open, and pacing managed for hours on the march.

**Hyper-Vigilance (5 or 6 cards)** represents an intense, adrenalized combat posture: weapons at the low ready, shields locked, ears straining at every shadow, and total focus fixed on an imminent threat. You are primed to strike or react the instant steel is drawn, but sustaining that level of acute tension rapidly exhausts your mind and body.

### Tabletop Procedure

1. **Entering Hyper-Vigilance:** Outside of Crisis Time, you may deal additional cards face down from the top of your deck into your [[Vigilance]] pool, raising it to **5 or 6 cards** (including up to 1 readied card as normal).
2. **The Accelerated 30-Minute Clock:** Whenever your [[Vigilance]] pool is set above 4 cards, you must pay for vigilance **roughly every 30 in-game minutes** (instead of every hour):
   - Discard your entire [[Vigilance]] pool to your expended pile. _(To retain a readied card, discard 2 cards from the top of your deck to your expended pile instead)._
   - Deal cards face down from your deck back to your chosen Hyper-Vigilance level (5 or 6).
3. **Entering Crisis Time:** When danger erupts into [[Crisis Time]], you pick up your entire [[Vigilance]] pool into your hand for Round 1:
   - At Vigilance 5: You hold 5 cards + 2 cards drawn in the Plan Step = **7 cards in hand**.
   - At Vigilance 6: You hold 6 cards + 2 cards drawn in the Plan Step = **8 cards in hand** (one-third of your starting deck!).
4. **Easing Off:** You may voluntarily drop your [[Vigilance]] pool back down to 4 or lower at any time outside of crisis. Unneeded cards are discarded to your expended pile. Once you return to 4 or fewer cards, your payment interval returns to **roughly once per in-game hour** starting from that moment.

## Gamemaster's Guide

### The Philosophy of Hyper-Vigilance

Hyper-Vigilance is an opt-in risk engine. It gives players a high-leverage choice when approaching known peril:

- **The Reward (Tactical Dominance):** Starting Round 1 with 7 or 8 cards allows characters to immediately establish defensive stances, execute expensive Action Stacks, or unleash coordinated alpha-strikes before opponents can seize the initiative.
- **The Cost (Rapid Attrition):** Maintaining a pool of 6 cards removes one-quarter of a character's deck from circulation. Combined with paying 6 cards every 30 minutes (12 cards per hour), a 24-card deck empties and triggers a [[Fatigue Cycle]] in **exactly 2 hours** of continuous watch, even with zero combat or obstacle checks.

### Pacing and Table Ergonomics

A major reason the core rules use a 1-hour interval is tabletop ergonomics: tracking 30-minute intervals during long overland travel creates unnecessary table friction.

Hyper-Vigilance completely avoids this friction because **it is self-limiting**:

- During overland travel, players will almost never choose Hyper-Vigilance because marching for 6 hours at Vigilance 6 would burn 72 cards, cycling their decks three times and crippling them with 6 to 12 [[Fatigue]] cards.
- The 30-minute clock **only activates in tense, short-duration scenes**: breaching an enemy stronghold, holding a barricaded room, standing a high-threat midnight sentry shift, or creeping through a monster lair.

### Interaction with Gear and Burden

Hyper-Vigilance is especially punishing for armored characters:

- Heavy plate or bulky kit adds [[Burden]] to the [[Fatigue Cycle]].
- An unarmored rogue at Vigilance 6 takes 2 Fatigue cards every 2 hours.
- A knight in heavy plate (Burden 2) at Vigilance 6 takes $2 + 2 = 4$ [[Fatigue]] cards every 2 hours. After a single 4-hour high-alert crawl, that knight's deck is diluted with 8 Fatigue cards (one-third of their entire deck!), vividly modeling the crushing physical and thermal exhaustion of wearing a full harness in combat readiness.

### Verisimilitude & Grounding

This module directly reflects findings in cognitive psychology and military operations:

- **The Vigilance Decrement:** Laboratory research (Mackworth, 1948; Klein & Feltmate, 2025 [`klein-2025`](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/klein-2025-vigilance-decrement-75-years/extracted.md)) proves that human perceptual sensitivity ($d'$) drops significantly within 20 to 30 minutes of continuous monitoring.
- **Resource Depletion:** High-alert scanning is an active consumer of prefrontal cognitive resources, elevating sympathetic distress hormones (Al-Shargie et al., 2019 [`esteban-2019`](file:///home/tdimiduk/cardpg/cardpg/design/research/sources/verisimilitude/esteban-2019-vigilance-decrement-review/extracted.md)).
- **Sentry Tours:** Historical and modern military doctrine (U.S. Army FM 22-6 / TC 3-22.6) limits active sentry posts to **2 hours** because longer shifts lead to severe attentional failure.

## Examples in Play

### Example 1: Breaching the Crypt Door

The party is stacked outside the iron doors of an ancient crypt. They hear chanting and the scraping of iron claws within.

1. **Setting Hyper-Vigilance:** Before opening the door, the Ranger and Warrior choose to push to **Vigilance 6**. They deal cards from their decks until each has a readied weapon action and 5 face-down cards on the table.
2. **The Breach:** The Rogue picks the lock (resolving as a Casual Attempt with blind flips to save cards). The doors swing open, revealing a crypt-ghoul priest and three thralls.
3. **Round 1 Hand:** Both the Ranger and Warrior pick up their 6 cards into hand and draw their 2 round cards, starting Round 1 with **8 cards**.
4. **Resolution:** The Ranger plays a Cost 2 Action Stack to loose a volley of two arrows immediately; the Warrior establishes an active Shield Stance and intercepts a charging thrall. They dominate the opening exchange.
5. **The Aftermath:** The fight lasts 3 rounds. When tension breaks, both discard unused hand cards. Because they spent roughly 15 minutes of in-game time in Hyper-Vigilance, the GM advances the clock: if they remain at Vigilance 6 for another 15 minutes as they search the crypt, they will have to pay their 6-card Hyper-Vigilance payment. Deciding the immediate threat is resolved, both players ease off to **Vigilance 2**, returning to the standard 1-hour interval.

### Example 2: The Sentry on the Parapet

During a siege, the GM establishes that enemy sappers or assassins might scale the wall at any moment.

- The scout stands a **2-hour watch** at **Vigilance 6** with an arrow readied.
- At the 30-minute mark, the scout pays 6 cards.
- At the 60-minute mark, the scout pays 6 cards (12 total).
- At the 90-minute mark, the scout pays 6 cards (18 total).
- At the 120-minute mark (2 hours), the scout pays 6 cards (24 total).
- **The Fatigue Cycle:** The scout's 24-card deck is completely exhausted by the 2 hours of intense scanning. The scout adds 2 [[Fatigue]] cards, reshuffles, and is relieved by the next sentry. If an assassin struck at minute 110, the scout had 6 cards in hand ready to shoot—but was physically and mentally at the very limit of their endurance.
