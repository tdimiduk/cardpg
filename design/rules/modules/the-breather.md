This module introduces an optional rule for Adventuring Time called **The Breather** (or Short Rest). It provides a lightweight mechanic for players to spend narrative time (10–15 minutes) to recover stamina and reshuffle their decks with **proportional fatigue**, rather than suffering the full flat penalty of an instant Fatigue Cycle.

---

## **Player Rules**

### **What is a Breather?**

In the core rules, a Fatigue Cycle can be triggered voluntarily at any moment to reshuffle your expended cards, but doing so always inflicts the full penalty of adding 2 `Fatigue` cards (plus your Burden) to your deck.

A **Breather** is a deliberate pause of approximately **10 to 15 minutes** in a location of relative safety. During a breather, your characters sit down, catch their breath, sip water, bind scrapes, and organize their gear.

In exchange for spending this narrative time, you perform a **Proportional Fatigue Cycle** that scales the fatigue cost to how depleted your deck actually is.

### **The Breather Procedure**

When the party takes a Breather, each player resolves the following steps:

1. **Count Expended Cards:** Count the cards currently in your expended pile.
2. **Determine Proportional Fatigue:**
   - **Minor Exertion (1 to 11 cards expended):** Add **1 Fatigue** card to your expended pile.
   - **Heavy Exertion (12 or more cards expended):** Add **2 Fatigue** cards to your expended pile.
3. **Burden Relief (Unbuckling Harness):** If you take off a heavy pack or unbuckle your armor harness during the 15-minute rest, **ignore your Burden** for this cycle! (If you must stay fully strapped and helmeted because danger is imminent, add your Burden to the fatigue cards above as normal).
4. **Reshuffle:** Reshuffle your entire expended pile back into your draw deck.
5. **Reset Guard:** You may search your freshly reshuffled deck to **Set Guard** with 1 Action Card face up for free. Deal the rest of your Ready Hand face down up to your chosen vigilance level.

---

## **Advanced Variant: Tactical Recall ("Second Wind")**

Some campaigns or specific character archetypes may want a way to retrieve a single exhausted technique without reshuffling the entire deck. GMs can offer this as an optional universal maneuver or print it on specific Trait/Stance cards.

- **Tactical Recall Maneuver:**
  During a brief pause outside of combat, you may strain to recall a spent maneuver. Search your expended pile for **1 Action Card** and place it face up as your guard (or into your Ready Hand).
  **The Cost:** You must immediately discard **2 cards from the top of your deck** into your expended pile to pay for the physical strain.

## **Gamemaster's Guide**

### **The Philosophy of the Breather**

The Breather is a pacing tool that turns **time** into a tangible resource. In the core rules, an instant Fatigue Cycle costs cards, but zero in-game time. A Breather flips the equation: it saves cards by spending in-game time.

This trade-off is only meaningful if time actually matters. A Breather should never be "free."

### **The Environmental Cost of 15 Minutes**

Whenever the party declares a Breather in an active environment (such as a dungeon, hostile wilderness, or enemy fortress), you should impose the natural consequences of 15 minutes passing:

- **Wandering Threats:** Make a wandering monster or hazard check (typically 1-in-6 on a d6, or flipping a card from an encounter deck).
- **Clocks and Fronts:** If you are using the [Clocks & Fronts](clocks-and-fronts.md) module, advance an active environmental or enemy clock by 1 tick.
- **Resource Depletion:** Torches burn down, lantern oil is consumed, or environmental conditions (rising water, creeping cold, toxic fumes) worsen.
- **Lost Initiative:** If the party is pursuing a fleeing enemy or trying to prevent a ritual, the enemy uses that 15 minutes to reinforce barricades, set ambushes, or complete preparations.

### **Safe Havens vs. Hostile Territory**

- **In a Safe Haven (Tavern, Secured Campsite, Sanctum):** The party can take breathers with zero threat of interruption. This makes safe havens feel distinct and valuable.
- **In an Unsecured Dungeon Hallway:** Taking a breather is a calculated risk. The players must weigh whether recovering cards is worth the risk of being ambushed while their armor is loosened and their guard is relaxed.

### **Example in Play**

> The party has just survived a harrowing skirmish with cavern ghouls. The warrior has 8 cards in her expended pile and wears heavy plate (Burden 2).
>
> - _Under Core Rules:_ If she triggers an instant Fatigue Cycle to recover her expended cards, she adds $2 + 2 = 4$ `Fatigue` cards to her deck immediately.
> - _Using the Breather Module:_ The party barricades the iron door of a crypt and takes a 15-minute Breather. The warrior unbuckles her breastplate and sits against the sarcophagus. Because she spent fewer than 12 cards, she only adds **1 Fatigue** card, and because she unstrapped her armor, her Burden is ignored.
>
> However, during those 15 minutes, the GM rolls for wandering monsters. The roll indicates an encounter: ghouls outside begin scratching at the iron door. The party recovered their cards cleanly, but the dungeon did not wait for them.
