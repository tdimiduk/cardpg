# Thinking With Your Deck: The Narrative of the Cards

Your deck is more than a resource; it's the story of your character's current state. Understanding the narrative behind the cards can help you make more intuitive and effective tactical decisions.

### Your Hand is Your Agency

An `Action Card` in your hand doesn't just represent your ability to do something; it represents a fleeting **tactical opening** that your character has perceived. Holding a `Trip` card means you are anticipating a moment of weakness. When you play that card, you are not asking the GM if you can trip someone; you are spending your agency to declare, "The opening to trip this foe happens _now_."

Your hand is a set of narrative possibilities that you have the power to unleash. The cards you play to fuel an action are just as important. Each one represents a different opportunity—a feint, a parry, a quick step—that you are consciously sacrificing to channel all your effort into a single, decisive maneuver.

### The Two Costs of Fatigue

[[Fatigue]] is the most common enemy in the game, and it attacks you in two distinct ways, modeling the dual nature of exhaustion:

- **The Cognitive Cost:** The cost is the draw itself. Where you should have drawn a new tactical option or perceived a battlefield opportunity, your exhausted mind came up blank. The [[Fatigue]] card you put into your hand is the tangible result of that mental stumble, representing a failure to find an opening or formulate a complex plan.
- **The Physical Cost:** When you play a [[Fatigue]] card as part of an action's cost or flip it during a defense, its low value (1) directly degrades the action's effectiveness. This models the physical reality of exhaustion: your mighty blow becomes weaker, your parry becomes clumsier, and your aim wavers.

# Advanced Tactics

This guide explores some of the deeper strategies and mechanical interactions in the game to help you make more informed tactical decisions.

## The Art of Hand Management: The High-Risk, High-Reward Gamble

The core rules warn of the "Risk of a Large Hand." This is not a bug, but a central, high-stakes tradeoff in the game's design. While hoarding cards leaves you vulnerable to a **Defensive Collapse**, it is also the primary way to set up the game's most powerful and decisive moments. A large hand represents a deep state of focus, preparation, or patience.

Understanding _why_ you would take this risk is key to mastering the game. It can represent a variety of things:

### 1. Powering Up

You are gathering your physical energy and mental focus for one overwhelming action. By holding many cards, you are preparing to unleash a "Mighty Blow" or a complex spell with such high [[Strength]] that it will dominate the round, forcing your opponent into a desperate position. This is the ultimate offensive gamble, sacrificing immediate flexibility for the chance to dictate the flow of the fight.

### 2. Opportunistic Patience

You are holding a hand full of versatile answers, waiting for the perfect moment to strike. Like a rogue waiting in the shadows, you aren't committed to a single plan but are prepared for multiple possibilities. This allows you to adapt to the flow of battle and unleash the perfect, high-impact action—like a "Sneak Attack"—at the moment it will be most effective.

### 3. Defensive Readiness

If your large hand is full of [[Defend]] cards and other reactive abilities, you are not setting yourself up for a collapse—you are preparing to weather what comes. This is a calculated defensive posture. You are inviting your opponent's best shot, confident that you can absorb the blow. This leaves them overextended and vulnerable, creating an opening for you to counter-attack on the following round when their own hand is depleted.

## The Art of Hand Management: Strain & Collapse

The core-rules.md warns of the "Risk of a Large Hand". Understanding the precise mechanics of this risk is key to managing your resources effectively. Holding many cards creates two distinct dangers:

1. Intense Focus Strain: This is the gradual cost of holding a complex plan in your mind. The more cards you hold, the fewer cards are in your deck and expended pile. This means each Fatigue Cycle gives you a smaller deck, causing you to fatigue faster over time as you maintain intense focus.

2. Defensive Collapse: This is a catastrophic failure state that can occur if you are attacked while holding many cards. The sequence is as follows:

- You begin flipping cards to defend, but your ready deck is too small. You exhaust it and trigger a Fatigue Cycle.

- The cards you already flipped for this defense do not get reshuffled, making your new ready deck incredibly small.

- You continue flipping, but now you are drawing the new, low-value Fatigue cards, rapidly increasing the defense's [[Impact]].

- If you exhaust the deck again, you will be forced to chain fatigue cycles, adding two Fatigue cards and flipping them until the attack's [[Strength]] is finally met.

This will result in a massive [[Impact]] and devastating consequences, likely taking you out of the fight while simultaneously flooding your deck with Fatigue. Hoarding cards is the ultimate gamble—it allows you to set up the perfect move, but leaves you perilously brittle if you are caught off guard.

## The Art of Vigilance: Managing Adventuring Time

Outside of combat, your deck functions as your party's stamina and endurance clock. How you manage your [[Vigilance]] pool during exploration dictates both your immediate readiness for sudden violence and your long-term stamina.

### The Vigilance Dial (0 to 4 Cards)

Your Vigilance pool size (from 0 to 4 cards) is not a static number—it is a tactical dial you adjust based on the danger of your surroundings:

- **Raising Vigilance Anytime:** If you hear eerie noises, enter an unmapped dungeon wing, or take point on watch, you can deal additional cards from your deck to your Vigilance pool at any time (up to 4). Remember that cards dealt to the table are committed stamina: when it is time to pay for vigilance, your entire Vigilance pool will flush to the expended pile.
- **Lowering Vigilance at the Flush:** Once dealt, cards cannot be returned to your draw deck. If you want to drop your vigilance to conserve stamina (e.g., reaching a secure campsite or safe road), you simply choose a lower size (like 0) when paying for vigilance.
- **Peak Alertness (4 Cards):** You are walking on hair-trigger alert, eyes scanning every shadow. If an ambush strikes, you pick up a full 4-card tactical hand. However, maintaining this level of tension is exhausting: each time you pay for vigilance flushes 4 cards into your expended pile. In a standard 24-card deck, maintaining peak alertness triggers a full Fatigue Cycle roughly every 6 hours.
- **Marching at Ease (0 Cards):** Packs are slung comfortably, and you are relaxing or chatting. Paying for vigilance costs **zero cards**—you can travel, explore, or rest indefinitely without fatiguing your deck. But if combat breaks out, you enter Crisis Time with an empty hand, spending Round 1 scrambling to react.
- **Moderate Vigilance (1–3 Cards):** A middle ground for extended exploration through uncertain territory, balancing reaction speed against deck attrition.

### The Burden of Plate

Because a Fatigue Cycle adds **2 + Burden** [[Fatigue]] cards to your deck, heavily armored characters pay a much steeper price for high vigilance. An unencumbered scout (Burden 0) gains 2 Fatigue per cycle, while a knight in full harness (Burden 3) gains 5 Fatigue per cycle!

This creates a natural, historical dynamic: knights take off their helms, rest their shields on pack horses, and lower their vigilance when not actively anticipating combat, while light scouts take point on high alert.

### Tactics of the Readied Card

Readying a card (spending ~1 minute to search your deck for 1 card to place face up in your Vigilance pool) gives you guaranteed tactical readiness. This can be an Action card—like priming a heavy attack or a defensive parry before kicking down the door—or simply a high-value card for sudden Snap Checks.

However, holding a readied card has a continuous cost: each time you pay for vigilance, you must discard 2 cards off the top of your deck to keep it.

- **When to hold it:** When an encounter is imminent (e.g., breaching a room, stalking a patrol).
- **When to let it go:** During long marches or when the path ahead is clear. Letting it flush back to discard saves your deck from rapid exhaustion.

### The Strategy of General Actions

When faced with a General Action (climbing a cliff, negotiating a toll, picking a lock), you face a fundamental choice: **Casual Attempt** vs. **Committed Attempt**.

#### 1. Casual Attempt

You do not touch your Vigilance pool; you resolve the task purely by flipping cards from your deck.

- **The Upside:** Your current Vigilance pool remains untouched on the table, preserving any readied card or high reaction cards you may have waiting.
- **The Risk:** Blind flips have natural variance. If you hit low values, you may flip many cards, driving up [[Impact]] and taking more consequences.

#### 2. Committed Effort

You pick up your Vigilance pool, bringing your conscious focus, trained techniques, and immediate physical energy to bear on the problem.

- **Hand Cards in the Flipped Stack:** When you add a card from your hand to the flipped stack in place of a flip, it counts toward the task's [[Impact]] just like a card flipped from your deck. This prevents players from dumping weak cards: playing a card with `Yellow 1` adds 1 full Impact for negligible Strength.
- **The Golden Rule: Never Play Low-Value Cards to the Flipped Stack!**
  A blind flip from your deck has an average value (typically ~3). If your hand holds a low-value card, let it flush away! Only add cards from your hand to the flipped stack that significantly beat your deck's average (e.g., `Yellow 5` or higher).
- **The Flush:** When the action resolves, any cards remaining in your hand are discarded to your expended pile, and you deal fresh cards to your Vigilance pool back up to your chosen level. Committing your hand doesn't leave you defenseless—your Vigilance pool is redrawn immediately—but it represents cycling through your current focus and stamina to overcome the obstacle.

_(Note: If your table plays with the [Selective Commitment Module](modules/selective-commitment.md), you may choose to commit a measured subset of your Vigilance pool rather than picking up the entire hand)._

#### Playing Printed Action Stacks

If your hand contains a relevant Action Card, you can play it as an [[Action Stack]] (with additional cards from hand equal to its printed [[Cost]]).

- **Action Stacks Do Not Count for Impact:** Just as declaring an attack in combat does not inflict self-harm, cards in your Action Stack do not go into the flipped stack. Instead, the action alters the terms of the challenge: providing bonus [[Strength]], establishing an active [[Defense]], enabling cross-color spending, or granting specific protections.
- **Example: Jumping the Chasm with Athletics:**
  The Swashbuckler faces a dangerous 10-meter leap (`Strength 18 Yellow`). Rather than risking a blind leap, they spend a minute of preparation to **Ready** `Athletics` (Cost 1, `Yellow 4`, text: _+2 Strength, Defense 2 on physical maneuvers_), searching their deck to place it face up as their readied card in their Vigilance pool.
  When they make the jump, they choose a Committed Attempt and declare `Athletics`, playing it into an [[Action Stack]] with 1 additional card from hand (e.g. `Yellow 3`) to meet its printed Cost of 1.
  The Action Stack establishes `Defense 2` and contributes $4\text{ (Athletics)} + 3\text{ (supporting card)} + 2\text{ (modifier)} = 9$ Yellow Strength!
  To meet the remaining 9 Strength, they add one high-Yellow card (`Yellow 5`) from hand to the flipped stack in place of a flip, and flip one card from the deck (`Yellow 4`). Total Strength is $9 + 5 + 4 = 18$.
  The flipped stack contains exactly 2 cards (`Impact = 2`). With `Defense 2`, they suffer $2 / 2 = 1$ minor scrape instead of risking 5 or 6 blind flips. When the jump is complete, their hand flushes, and they deal a new Vigilance pool face down.
